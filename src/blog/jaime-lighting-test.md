My friend <span class="h-card p-name">Jaime Ladd</span> coördinates an “adventurous diners” group on a popular social media platform; one of the group members, <span class="h-card p-name">Waco Tomlinson</span>, recently killed a wild boar and offered up some choice cuts to the other members to prepare individual dishes for a potluck luncheon. Jaime agreed to deliver a shoulder to me so I could make red chili <span lang="es">pozole</span>, so I asked if she’d mind modeling for me as I continue to work on my studio lighting technique.

For the shoot, I used a Paul C. Buff “Einstein” (model E640) studio strobe and metered with a Sekonic L-308S Flashmate exposure meter. The light was positioned as close to the model as possible without encroaching the frame, then shot through a 43&Prime; Westcott white umbrella. The strobe was set to a very low setting to achieve light falloff behind the subject, leaving the black backdrop mostly unlit.

We used whatever props were at hand&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;a metal pitcher filled with colorful cloth napkins, a brown stoneware jug, a vintage bourbon bottle, a book&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;and just had fun with it. I hope you like the results.
