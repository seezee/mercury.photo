I spent the next couple of months obsessively researching Leica cameras and lenses. I began by downloading the [owner’s manual](https://www.cameramanuals.org/leica_pdf/leica_m3.pdf){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} for the M3 rangefinder. Then I practiced composing and focusing with no film in the camera.

I quickly realized that the lens that <span class="h-card p-name p-given-name">Joe</span> had given me was impractical: the frame lines were too restrictive, and, even with the M3 viewfinder’s high magnification,[^1] it was simply too hard to focus accurately. It was also big (5.019&Prime; long) and heavy (just shy of 1 lb. or 0.45 kg.)

[^1]: The M3 viewfinder’s magnification is .91×, almost life-sized. Compare that to today’s M9, with its .58× viewfinder.

Later reading revealed that the lens, a Hektor 135mm ƒ/4.5 manufactured circa <time datetime="1955">1955</time>, is the [least loved lens](https://www.kenrockwell.com/leica/135mm-f45.htm){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} Leica ever produced.

<blockquote cite="https://www.kenrockwell.com/leica/135mm-f45.htm">
  
  Leica has an almost 100-year history of making many of the world’s very finest photographic lenses. [The Hektor 135<abbr>mm</abbr> ƒ/4.5] isn’t one of them.
  
  <footer>&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;<cite class="cite-person">Ken Rockwell</span></footer>
</blockquote>

After a little poking about the dusty corners of the internet, I found <span class="h-card p-name">Ken Rockwell</span>’s blog. Of particular interest is the section on [Leica lenses](https://www.kenrockwell.com/leica/lens-reviews.htm){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. These are thorough, detailed, highly opinionated reviews, and required reading for anyone considering shooting with a **Leica** (or any other rangefinder, for that matter). Also informative were <span class="h-card p-name">Steve Huff</span>’s [reviews](https://stevehuffphoto.com){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and CameraQuest’s [guide](https://www.cameraquest.com/mlenses.htm){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} to M-mount lenses.

My first instinct was to go cheap and buy **Voigtländer** (sometimes called **Cosina**, after the brand’s owner, or abbreviated as CV). Ken quickly [changed my mind](https://www.kenrockwell.com/leica/cosina.htm){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. But I couldn’t afford the Leica glass, either, especially since the price of used Leica lenses began to inflate after the introduction of the M8, Leica’s first digital rangefinder, in <time datetime="2006-09">2006</time>. Fortunately, Cosina manufactures lenses under the **Zeiss** brand name that are comparable in quality to Leica but at prices similar to Voigtländer. More on that later.

I spent a lot of time adding Leica and Zeiss lenses to my eBay watch list after that, thinking I’d buy a couple of lenses, and that would be that. But it was not to be. Remember the [Dutchman](http://photosteron.com){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and his crazy digital Leica? I still lusted for the M9, but the price was pretty scary. Then I stumbled on <span class="h-card p-name">Thorsten Overgaard</span>’s blog.

Overgaard is a Danish writer and photojournalist [who had written](https://www.overgaard.dk/leica-M9-digital-rangefinder-camera.html){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} extensively on his experience with the Leica M9 when it was released in 2009. He was one of the first professionals to use it, obtaining his camera a mere two days after its official release.

I’ve always been one of those people who has to have the very best, and, as you’d expect, I’m rarely happy very long with my personal possessions&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;something about green grass and fences.[^2] So I would be taking an expensive risk if I bought the camera and became disenchanted with it. Overgaard’s review convinced me to take that risk.

[^2]: Psychologists call this hedonic adaptation.

I read his entire, multi-page essay[^3] on the camera, with particular interest in:

[^3]: The essay continues to grow as Overgaard adds to it whenever Leica releases a new model of their flagship digital rangefinder. <time datetime="2025-10-15">As of this update,</time> the essay comprises a whopping sixty pages.

* The camera’s menu & recommended settings
* Deployment (set white balance, set lens wide-open, set aperture priority, shoot only <abbr title="International Organization for Standardization">ISO</abbr> 160 or ISO 800 most of the time)
* Adobe Lightroom workflow

These particular sections of the essay convinced me that the M9 is actually a pretty simple camera to use, unlike most <abbr info-data="digital single lens reflex camera">DSLR</abbr>s and even many point-and-shoots on the market. That made it easier for me to rationalize the purchase.[^4] The overall essay is insightful and persuasive, so if you’re on the fence (and have a lot of time on your hands), [go read it](https://www.overgaard.dk/leica-M9-digital-rangefinder-camera.html){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

[^4]: Who am I kidding? I wanted the camera and did the research to justify a decision I had already made.
