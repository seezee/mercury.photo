We first met singer-songwriter <span class="h-card p-name">Alex Culbreth</span> in <time datetime="2013-07-12T16:00-05:00">July</time>, when he was featured in a Woody Guthrie Folk Festival solo performance at the Brick Street Café. Although it was his first official showcase at the festival, he’d attended the four previous years, singing and playing around the campfires through the wee hours.

A veteran of the alt-country scene, Culbreth has opened for (among others) <span class="h-card p-org">[Rusted Root](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/rusted-root/id260202?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span> and <span class="h-card p-org">[Spirit Family Reunion](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/spirit-family-reunion/id544465875?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span>. <cite>No Depression,</cite> <cite>Common Folk Music</cite>, <cite>Americana Music Show</cite>, and <cite>Ninebullets Radio</cite> have all featured him. Culbreth dropped in <time datetime="2013-11-27T16:45-06:00">the day before Thanksgiving</time> for a drink and a chat about his current six month&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&ndash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;long solo tour. Later that night Culbreth played an early set at JJ’s Alley in Oklahoma City. Then after spending Thanksgiving in Tulsa with some mutual acquaintances, he played a Black Friday set at the Bluebonnet Bar in Norman; we were on hand to photograph his set.

## Interview: Alex Culbreth

<span class="spkr">Mercury Photo Bureau</span> Welcome to Mercury Photo Bureau. I hear you’ve been on a mini tour?

<span class="spkr">Alex Culbreth</span> Yeah, [I’m] about two weeks into a six week tour. I’ve been through D.C., Tennessee, Ohio, and Oklahoma, now.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> How did you become interested in music?

<span class="spkr">Culbreth</span> Growin’ up, my mom played me a lot of <span class="h-card p-name">[Patsy Cline](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/patsy-cline/id95778?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span> and <span class="h-card p-name">[Louis Prima](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/louis-prima/id550748?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span>, so I was always listening to good music [&hellip;]. I don’t think I started playing until I was about thirteen; I actually wanted to be in a punk band.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I’ve met a lot of singer-songwriters, especially in the alt-country and roots scene, who started out in punk bands.

<span class="spkr">Culbreth</span> All throughout middle school and high school, all I listened to was punk. I liked the old L.A. stuff; <span class="h-card p-org">[X](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/x/1295432230?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span> and the <span class="h-card p-org">[Dead Kennedys](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/dead-kennedys/id6904654?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span> and the <span class="h-card p-org">Screamers</span> and the <span class="h-card p-org">[Germs](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/the-germs/id41456743?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span> , uh, the New York stuff&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;<span class="h-card p-name">[Richard Hell](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/richard-hell-the-voidoids/id7056645?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span>, <span class="h-card p-name">[Patti Smith](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/patti-smith/id13762?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span>, the <span class="h-card p-org">[New York Dolls](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/new-york-dolls/id81689?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span>. It wasn’t until I started listening to <span class="h-card p-name">[Billy Bragg](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/billy-bragg/id163251?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span> that I got into the whole singer-songwriter thing. ’cause he kinda found a cool marriage between the punk world and the folk world. There are a lot of similarities between folk and punk. I think <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-nickname">[Woody](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/woody-guthrie/id236313?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span> <span class="p-family-name">Guthrie</span></span>’s  about as punk as you can get.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> What was your first instrument?

<span class="spkr">Culbreth</span> Guitar. I picked it up for my thirteenth birthday, and I was just gonna try to play punk on an acoustic guitar [&hellip;]. I tried to start a lot of [punk bands], but they never seemed to pan out; [we] never seemed to be able to get together and make it happen. So, all the more reason why I started playing by myself. It was listening to Billy Bragg, and another guy, <span class="h-card p-name">[Patrick Fitzgerald](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/patrick-fitzgerald/id61020385?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span>. He was around in the late ’70s; he opened for the <span class="h-card p-org">[Clash](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/the-clash/id522000?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span> on a couple of tours, just playin’ punk on an acoustic guitar. He’d only play those three chords; he wasn’t a very good singer; but he wrote great lyrics, and so listening to him and Billy Bragg showed me that I could really do it by myself.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Do you remember how you discovered those guys?

<span class="spkr">Culbreth</span> I think I kind of worked backwards. I started listening to ’90s punk bands like <span class="h-card p-org">[Rancid](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/rancid/id1857507?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span> and just found out who *they* were influenced by; started listening to <span class="h-card p-org">[Bad Religion](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/bad-religion/id150160?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span> and <span class="h-card p-org">[Minor Threat](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/minor-threat/id49249573?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span>, ’80s punk, and kind of working backwards.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I know you’ve been in a couple of bands, as well as performing solo&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;the <span class="h-card p-org">[Parlor Soldiers](https://theparlorsoldiers.bandcamp.com/){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span> and the <span class="h-card p-org">[Dead Country Stars](https://deadcountrystars.bandcamp.com/){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span>.

<span class="spkr">Culbreth</span> [The Parlor Soldiers] started out as a duo project with me and a girl&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;<span class="h-card p-name">Karen Jonas</span>&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;who lived in Fredericksburg, Virginia. So we were both playing music in the same town, and we started writin’ songs together. She had more of a kind of folky, uh, <span class="h-card p-name">[Joni Mitchell](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/joni-mitchell/id203373?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span> influence to her. […] We did one album together […]. I met [the future members of the Dead Country Stars] in Fredericksburg; they were all playing in different bands. I started out playing with <span class="h-card p-name">Eddie Dickerson</span> on fiddle&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;he’s now living in Austin&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;<span class="h-card p-name">Ryan Hale</span> on drums, <span class="h-card p-name">Joanna Smith</span> on upright bass, and <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-nickname">Jimbo</span> <span class="p-family-name">Carrico</span></span> on banjo. As far as the name [goes], some of the best country stars *are* dead.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> In your solo act, you play guitar and sing, but you also play a kick-drum, which you play standing in front of with your heels in the pedals. I’ve only known one other drummer to do that&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;<span class="h-card p-name">Travis Searle</span> of the now defunct Norman band <span class="h-card p-org">[Shi++y Awesome](/blog/shitty-awesomes-final-bow/)</span>.

<span class="spkr">Culbreth</span> For some reason, that always seemed more comfortable to me&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;it seems like it’s easier to keep my balance when I’m on my toes with the kick-drum and the tambourine. I tried having them both in front before, and I just wasn’t able to keep my balance. There’s a couple of one-man bands that I’ve been listening to recently that have made me want to expand what I do, and shows me how much can be done, how much noise can come out of just one person. They’re both out of Austin: <span class="h-card p-name">Scott H. Biram</span> and <span class="h-card p-name">Shakey Graves</span>.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> What’s one song you’re really proud to have written?

<span class="spkr">Culbreth</span> I really like <cite class="short-work">Mercy Me.</cite> It’s a song about travelin’ and playing music and tryin’ not to let your head get too big. That’s one of the few songs where I try to fit a whole bunch of words into a small amount of space. Some of my favorite writers are like, <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-family-name">[Bukowski](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/charles-bukowski/id4271497?mt=11&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span></span> and <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-family-name">[Hemingway](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/ernest-hemingway/id2122513?mt=11&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span></span>, you know; those kind of “punchy” writers who say a lot in a small space. They can grab your shirt collar with just one sentence.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You have one album with the Dead Country Stars, <cite>Heart in a Mason Jar</cite>, on BandCamp&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;when did that come out?

<span class="spkr">Culbreth</span> That came out in <time datetime="2013">January</time>. I’ve got pretty much enough songs for a new album; I’m workin’ on a couple of new songs&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;[I’ll] see what shakes out. I’m moving right now; in a couple of month I’m moving to Austin. […] I’m moving because of the music scene and because of the food. I *love* Mexican food. [I plan to] keep on playin’, keep on travelin’ as much as I can. That’s what I’m doing right now, pretty much; touring full-time. [I plan to] keep on playin’, keep on travelin’ as much as I can. That’s what I’m doing right now, pretty much; touring full-time.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> What’s on your iPod?

<span class="spkr">Culbreth</span> <span class="h-card p-org">[Shovels &amp; Rope](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/shovels-rope/id524529368?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span>; they’ve been my favorite band for a couple of years now. The first time I saw them was in Charlottesville, Virginia; they opened for <span class="h-card p-name">[Hayes Carll](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/hayes-carll/id6668917?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span> and <span class="h-card p-name">[Jason Isbell](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/jason-isbell/id254571412?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span> from the <span class="h-card p-org">Drive-By Truckers</span> and they just blew me away. I mentioned to you earlier that I was listening to <cite>[Nighthawks at the Diner](https://music.apple.com/us/album/nighthawks-at-the-diner-2018-remaster/1485072511?mt=1&app=music&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite>, the <span class="h-card p-name">Tom Waits</span> album; so many great, punchy, beautiful lines; it’s like songwriter porn!

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Thanks for coming.

<span class="spkr">Culbreth</span> Thanks for having me!
