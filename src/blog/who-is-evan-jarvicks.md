<div class="drop-cap descender">

<span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">Jarvix</span>. The man. The musician. The mystery. Cynics call his music “twee.” We call it sensitive and bittersweet. Underneath all the lyrics flows a melancholy current blended with self-deprecating humor. <span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">Evan Jarvicks</span> came by the studio <time datetime="2016-08-28:T16:12-5:00">last week</time> for a short interview. **Learn <span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">Jarvix</span>’s secret identity in a Mercury Photo Bureau exclusive**.
</div>

<stack-l class="no-drop-cap">

<span class="spkr">Mercury Photo Bureau</span> Right before you got here, you were in the studio laying down some brass?

<span class="h-card p-name p-nickname spkr">Evan Jarvicks</span> Yeah, so Larry Chin has a compilation called <cite>[Nostalgia and the BrainSick Fever](https://outpost31studiosokc.bandcamp.com/album/nostalgia-and-the-brainsick-fever){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite> out on <time datetime="2016-09-02">September 2<sup>nd</sup></time>. <span class="h-card p-organization">Larry Chin</span> [^1] is one of <span class="h-card p-name">Kyle Mayfield</span>’s projects. He hit up a bunch of people[^2] who he wanted to work with.

[^1]: a.k.a. <span class="h-card p-organization">Larry Chin Is Dead</span>, a.k.a. <span class="h-card p-organization">Larry Chin Sleeps No More</span>.

[^2]: The 23-track album features 29 musicians, including <span class="h-card p-name">Morgan Hartman</span>, <span class="h-card p-name">Kyle Reid</span>, Jarvix, <span class="h-card p-organization">Oklahoma Cloud Factory</span>, <span class="h-card p-name">Sophia Macias</span> and many former members of Mayfield’s <span class="h-card p-organization">Ugly Suit</span>.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> This is not part of the <cite>[Fright Night Club](https://frightnightclub.bandcamp.com/){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite> series?

<span class="h-card p-nickname spkr">Jarvix</span> No, but it is part of the Larry Chin project. Kyle wrote many, but not all, of the songs on the album. It’s a collaboration.[^3] I play French Horn on one track and I have an electronic track I wrote and play on too. It features some woozy Theremin in the background. Kyle took what I laid down and added over it.

[^3]: The <cite>Fright Night Club</cite> series is also a collaboration among various musicians, mostly from Oklahoma.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Is it a real Theremin?

<span class="h-card p-nickname spkr">Jarvix</span> It’s a Moog Theremini. You play it like a regular Theremin, and it sounds like the real thing, but electronically, it’s a different instrument.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Tell us about your names. Let’s start with the name on your birth certificate.

<span class="h-card p-nickname spkr">Jarvix</span> My legal name is <span class="h-card p-name p-given-name">Steven</span>, which sounds like <span class="h-card p-name p-given-name">Evan</span>, but isn’t quite. <span class="h-card p-name">Steven Hardin</span>. It’s spelled the same way as a musician in Seattle, so if people try to track me down on the internet, they get that guy instead of me.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You perform as **Jarvix** and you go by <span class="h-card p-name">Evan Jarvicks</span>. The latter is a play on words?

<span class="h-card p-nickname spkr">Jarvix</span> Right. That’s how people know me. A lot of people do not know that that’s not my name. If you reverse Evan Jarvicks, you get <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-family-name"> Jarvicks</span> <span class="p-given-name">Evan</span></span>, which sounds like **Jarvik 7**, the first successfully transplanted artificial heart.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Describe your music.

<span class="h-card p-nickname spkr">Jarvix</span> I call it <q>Ukelele QuirkPop,</q> but that doesn’t encompass everything I write. I picked up my first "uke" about <time datetime="2011">5 years ago</time> and learned how to play from the internet. I learned it well enough that I could write songs, and I’ve written poetry all my life. Then, I had been messing around with some audio software, and it all kind of came together.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> For those who haven’t seen you perform, you use a particular technique and technology?

<span class="h-card p-nickname spkr">Jarvix</span> When I’m **live**, and that’s a specific thing that I do, I use a looping pedal to make live, on-the-fly, layered arrangements. I primarily play the ukelele, but I also play the bass when I need a bassline, and I make percussion with objects I borrow from the audience&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;car keys, credit cards, whatever’s in peoples’ pockets or purses.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> <time datetime="2016-08-06">Last show I saw you at</time>, there was quite a bit of improvising.

<span class="h-card p-nickname spkr">Jarvix</span> Yes, that’s something I’ve been working on diligently. I host an open-mic comedy night, so I get to try all kinds of weird stuff whenever I go up, and I’ve been improvising with the audience, crowd-sourcing everything from chord structure to time signature to tempo. Last week I solicited a song title and wound up pulling up <span class="h-card p-name">Britney Spears</span>’ <cite>[Toxic](https://geo.itunes.apple.com/us/album/toxic/id251947909?i=251948354&at=10ldhk&mt=1&app=music){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite> on my phone and sampling it for a loop&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;that’s the kind of thing I wind up doing.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Tell me about what you’re listening to now. What’s on your iPod?

<span class="h-card p-nickname spkr">Jarvix</span> I run the blog at Cellar Door Music Group, so I listen to a lot of local indie music. I try to spotlight bands and musicians who I think are being overlooked.[^4] [<span class="h-card p-name">Annie Ellicott</span>](https://geo.itunes.apple.com/jp/album/midnight-sun-feat.-annie-ellicott/id1111024136?i=1111024636&at=10ldhk&mt=1&app=music){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} is doing some terrific stuff&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;it’s experimental chamber-pop, but she has a jazz background. She performs a lot of jazz standards, but her debut is something totally different to that. I believe it comes out in <time datetime="2016-09">September</time>. Her [music videos](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCoUlL_KRAbRuHgEfFRkh22A){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} are really strange and atmospheric.

[^4]: Cellar Door Music Group closed its doors in <time datetime="2018">2018</time>. Currently, Jarvix can be found at [Make Oklahoma Weirder](https://www.makeoklahomaweirder.com/){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Time for Redeem-a-Song&trade;. What’s a song you think has gotten a bum rap, and convince my why I should appreciate it?

<span class="h-card p-nickname spkr">Jarvix</span> VH1’s <cite>50 Most Awesomely Bad Songs Ever</cite> was topped by [<span class="h-card p-organization">Jefferson Starship</span>’s] <cite>[We Built This City (on Rock and Roll)](https://music.apple.com/us/album/knee-deep-in-the-hoopla/1446293102?i=298007980&at=10ldhk&mt=1&app=music){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite>. I don’t necessarily defend the song&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;it’s pretty atrocious&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;but there’s enough there that I’m actually recording a cover of it. It’s a collaboration with a lot of other musicians. We’re recording it as a charity single for music education in Oklahoma, in light of budget cuts [to arts education under the present state leadership]. There’s a certain relevance to the lyrics when applied to the budget situation; arts funding has always been the hardest.

The single actually started life as part of something else. There’s a fellow in Guthrie, <span class="h-card p-name">Jeremy Hall</span>,[^5] who issued an internet challenge to make good covers of randomly assigned bad songs. But then nobody did it but me.

[^5]: Hall is one-half of the duo <span class="h-card p-organization">Welcome Little Stranger</span> and guitarist at <span class="h-card p-organization">Get Fired</span>.

I’ve taken some liberties with it. I slowed down the tempo and cut off the last half of the song&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;it only has two verses. But I think it still retains its integrity while hopefully excising the things that have made it so critically, uh, lambasted. I hope to have the single recorded and released soon.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Thank you so much for dropping by.

<span class="h-card p-nickname spkr">Jarvix</span> Oh, absolutely. It’s been wonderful.
