<div class="drop-cap">

One of the most popular recent broadcasts on KOSU the SPY FM’s <span class="h-card p-org">Oklahoma Rock Show</span> was their all <a href="https://www.amazon.com/gp/search?ie=UTF8&tag=mercphotamzn-20&linkCode=ur2&linkId=99791f85f7b6b13bc917436089cc93ed&camp=1789&creative=9325&index=music&keywords=roger miller" target="_blank" rel="external noopener"><span class="h-card p-name">Roger Miller</span></a> program. When local musician <span class="h-card p-name">Jerrod Beck</span> heard the show last <time datetime="2015-06">June</time>, he approached his friend, singer <span class="h-card p-name">Gabriel Knight Hancock</span>, with an idea. <q>What if we did a live Roger Miller cover show?</q> Hancock was on board, but it soon became apparent to both musicians that the show could, and should, be larger than the two of them.
</div><mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2015-10-30-smiths-tribute/czahller-20150927-19-49-31.jpg", "img-constrained", "Aaron Tomasko (upright bass) accompanying Rachel Brashear (keys & vox) at twilight", "Aaron Tomasko & Rachel Brashear (I)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

In a short time Beck and Hancock organized a benefit show at <a href="https://plantshoppe.com" target="_blank" rel="external noopener">The Plant Shoppe</a>, an Oklahoma City (you guessed it) plant shop known for hosting shows and workshops. The lineup grew to eight additional musicians and raised significant funds for shop owners <span class="h-card p-name">Jen Semmler</span> and <span class="h-card p-name">Chad Grubs</span>, who had suffered financial stress after their child was born prematurely.

As with a certain potato chip brand, one show proved not to be enough. Within a span of four months, Beck has organized three more tribute shows, this time on the Power House Bar’s patio stage. First came a <a href="https://www.amazon.com/gp/search?ie=UTF8&tag=mercphotamzn-20&linkCode=ur2&linkId=99791f85f7b6b13bc917436089cc93ed&camp=1789&creative=9325&index=music&keywords=john prine" target="_blank" rel="external noopener"><span class="h-card p-name">John Prine</span></a> tribute, then a <a href="https://www.amazon.com/gp/search?ie=UTF8&tag=mercphotamzn-20&linkCode=ur2&linkId=99791f85f7b6b13bc917436089cc93ed&camp=1789&creative=9325&index=music&keywords=the smiths" target="_blank" rel="external noopener">Smiths</a> tribute, and, this past <time data-="2015-10-25">Sunday</time>, a tribute to the late Oklahoma singer-songwriter <a href="https://www.amazon.com/gp/search?ie=UTF8&tag=mercphotamzn-20&linkCode=ur2&linkId=99791f85f7b6b13bc917436089cc93ed&camp=1789&creative=9325&index=music&keywords=jj cale" target="_blank" rel="external noopener"><span class="h-card p-name">J.J. Cale</span></a>.<mpb-dialog-img>

Mercury Photo Bureau was on hand to document the [Smiths tribute](#gallery-smiths-tribute) and the [J.J.Cale show](/blog/jj-cale-tribute/).

## Building community

Regarding the series’ mission, Beck said, <q>The shows are about paying tribute, but they are also about building community among musicians locally. We encourage every artist involved to collaborate with one another. Most times these local musicians and songwriters are busy doing their own shows and don’t get to see one another perform. The tribute shows create an opportunity for reunion and mutual appreciation for various styles of local song craft. It’s about having fun and it’s about growing as artists.</q>
