The city of Norman celebrated its thirtieth annual Jazz in June music festival <time datetime="2013-06-22">a couple of weeks ago</time> by shaking its hips to the funky grooves of New Orleans’ [<span class="h-card p-org">Dirty Dozen Brass Band</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/dirty-dozen-brass-band/id158578569?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. Led by <span class="h-card p-name">Roger Lewis</span> (soprano saxophone, baritone saxophone), the band’s lineup also featured <span class="h-card p-name">Gregory Davis</span> (trumpet), <span class="h-card p-name">Efrem Towns</span> (trumpet, flugelhorn), <span class="h-card p-name">Kirk Joseph</span> (sousaphone), and <span class="h-card p-name">Alvin Ford</span> (drums). Substituting on tenor saxophone and Hammond organ, respectively, were <span class="h-card p-name">Calvin Johnson, <abbr>Jr.</abbr></span> and <span class="h-card p-name">Richard Knox</span>.{.drop-cap}

The band’s set included numerous traditional New Orleans tunes, including <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-nickname">Professor Longhair</span></span>’s <cite class="short-work">[Tipitina](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/tipitina/id2486402?i=2486382&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite> and <cite class="short-work">[Mardis Gras in New Orleans](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/mardi-gras-in-new-orleans/id2675236?i=2675214&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"},</cite> <cite class="short-work">Jock-a-Mo</cite> <abbr>a.k.a.</abbr> <cite class="short-work">[Iko Iko](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/jock-a-mo/id3443393?i=3443187&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"},</cite> and <cite class="short-work">[<abbr>St.</abbr> James Infirmary Blues](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/st.-james-infirmary/id356690012?i=356690478&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"},</cite> as well as crowd pleasers <cite class="short-work">[Me Like It Like That](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/me-like-it-like-that/id193588499?i=193589462&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite> and <cite class="short-work">[Dirty Old Man](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/dirty-old-man/id513166812?i=513167452&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.</cite>

Audience participation was mandatory (not that anyone had to be strong-armed), with Towns inviting a bevy of bodacious beauties onto the stage to shake and shimmy. Bandleader Lewis kept to the shadows, mostly giving the limelight to his fellow players, but he finally cut loose on the aforementioned <cite class="short-work">Dirty Ol’ Man,</cite> wiggling his hips like a twenty-year-old and pantomiming the full lechery of the title character while simultaneously wailing away on the baritone. Twice, Towns thrilled the audience by playing the trumpet and the flugelhorn simultaneously.

About midway through the show, local trumpeter <span class="h-card p-name">Stan Engle</span> joined the band during a 22-minute medley ending with that old chestnut <cite class="short-work">[When the Saints Go Marching In](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/when-the-saints-go-marching-in/id513166812?i=513167451&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.</cite> Other highlights included <span class="h-card p-name">James Brown</span>’s <cite class="short-work">[Sex Machine](https://geo.itunes.apple.com/us/album/get-up-i-feel-like-being-a-sex-machine-pt-1-2/900721190?i=900721192&amp;mt=1&amp;app=music&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite> and the burlesque standard <cite class="short-work">[Harlem Nocturne](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/harlem-nocturne/id409592783?i=409592831&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.</cite>

Established in <time datetime="1977">1977</time> by <span class="h-card p-name">Benny Jones</span> and members of the <span class="h-card p-org">Tornado Brass Band</span>, the world famous Dirty Dozen Brass Band revolutionized New Orleans brass band music by fusing elements of funk, R&B, and bebop jazz with traditional styles. They last played Norman’s Deli in <time datetime="2001">2001</time>. Here’s to hoping they won’t stay away so long in the future.
