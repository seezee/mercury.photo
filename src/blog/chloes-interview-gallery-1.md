# {{ title }}

<!-- markdownlint-enable MD025 --><mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "featured", "", "The Chloës pose on the steps of Wesley Church across the street from Kamp’s 1310 Lounge", "Tiffany, Lysandra, Brandie, April, & Leila", "eager" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

We spent most of [<abbr title="Norman Music Festival 6">NMF6</abbr>](https://normanmusicfestival.com){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} running from venue to venue photographing bands, but managed to squeeze in some time with the <span class="h-card p-org">Chloës</span> at their RV on Saturday afternoon. Present were keyboardist <span class="h-card p-name">Lysandra Chapman</span>, bassist <span class="h-card p-name">Brandie Dawson</span>, lead guitarist <span class="h-card p-name">Tiffany Byrd,</span> and drummer <span class="h-card p-name">Leila Wright</span>. Singer and writer <span class="h-card p-name">April Wenzel</span> was off recharging her psychic batteries[^1] following her scorching performance at Bill and Dee’s Tavern the previous evening&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;a performance that ended with most of the audience&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;men and women alike&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;pogoing and chanting the refrain from <cite class="short-work">[Put that Dick Away](https://music.apple.com/us/album/put-that-dick-away/1289963239?i=1289963247&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite>&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;more on that song in [part three](/blog/chloes-interview-gallery-3/){rel=next}.

[^1]:She was sleeping off a hangover in the RV.

After setting up our equipment and mixing up some tasty martinis for the band, we settled into a camp chair and conducted one of the easiest interviews ever&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;the ladies needed no prompting, and we asked very few "canned" questions. Unfortunately, we also forgot to press the record button. The Chloës very graciously let me interview them a *second* time&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;all in all, we spent almost four hours hanging out, mostly just listening while we sipped beer and enjoyed the afternoon.

<span class="spkr">Mercury Photo Bureau</span> How did the band start?

<span class="spkr">Brandie Dawson</span> April and I […] had some mutual friends and we met at a Hallowe’en party, and [we] started talking about bands. And I had played bass in a couple of bands before […]. I had not played for a while, and I was fine with that. Somehow, my boyfriend ratted me out, that I played bass, to April, ’cause she wanted to start a band. And we exchanged numbers and eventually, we got in touch with each other and just started […] playing, like, <span class="h-card p-org">Foo Fighters</span>&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;we didn’t know, like, <q>What do we do? I don’t know; we don’t have any songs,</q> and then we thought, <q>Well, we’ll find a drummer.</q> So we [posted an ad on] Craigslist, found a drummer&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;<span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Brittany</span></span>, who’s&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;we have a new drummer, of course: Leila, now&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;we had another keyboardist, <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Roxanna</span></span>. Both of [them], the drummer and the keyboardist, had&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;life [got] in the way, and […] they had to drop out. And […] we found Lysandra; April and Lysandra are really good friends&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Lysandra Chapman</span> Yeah, April and I were friends. I think I said before that April was taking [guitar] lessons, and I had known her for years and years and years, and&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;this is actually a funny story that I won’t mention&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Oh, please do.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> We may, or may not, have dated the same person, as my husband says. [*laughter*] No, we dated the same guy. She dated him very briefly, and I&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;back fifteen years ago&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;So we’d known each other for a long time. She was an army brat and had just finished college at Tulane and [had] come up to Oklahoma City […].

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-05-13-chloes-kamps/czahller-20121201-23-09-45.jpg", "", "April Wenzel seen from a. low angle. She is singing into a microphone. Her right hand is extended; she is pointing with her index finger.", "April (III)", "large" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

So I re-met her, and got to be friends with her again, ten years later, maybe twelve years later. She was kind of at a point in her [marriage] that, <q>We’re settled in, and I’m gonna start […] taking lessons, and I want to do this band thing; I’m gonna do it.</q> And I really admired that, ’cause I knew that she was a good guitarist, but she was taking guitar lessons! And I had grown up in band, and I was, like, <q>Wow! Someone actually […] takes lessons when they’re an adult&#8253; What is **that**?</q> […] I didn’t get that. But she did, and when she was in piano lessons, she met Roxanne, and Roxanne wrote some great stuff, and, yeah, life got in the way, and she couldn’t do it; she couldn’t be there, and so [April] asked me to be in the band, and I said&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You said [earlier] that you still use some of Roxanne’s riffs.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Oh yeah absolutely. <cite class="short-work">Go Out with Me</cite>; there’s a couple songs that are older songs&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Dawson</span> Yeah, the older songs. And some of them, we don’t really play any more.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Yeah, but still, if I play them, [the riffs] are definitely Roxanne’s. And she’s come out to a couple of gigs. And, like I said before, I didn’t want to stay in the band; I thought that I would just be very "interim," […] ’cause I’ve never really played piano; I’d taken brief lessons, but I played flute growing up, and I played trumpet; some weird […] instruments that were *not* gonna be in this band&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Dawson</span> We’ve brought in the flute before.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> That’s in the song <cite class="short-work">Put That Flute Away</cite>? [*laughter*]

<span class="spkr">Dawson</span> <cite class="short-work">Put That Flute Away.</cite> Right. Which did not have as much influence on our audience as <cite class="short-work">Put That Dick Away,</cite> so we scrapped it.

<span class="spkr">Leila Wright</span> I wanted to say how, [under April’s leadership], the focus has been very supportive of having room to grow&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Dawson</span> You don’t have to be an expert musician; I mean, we were not&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Wright</span>&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;and she sees the potential&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Oh, absolutely; she said to me, she said, <q>Oh, Girl!</q>&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;’cause I can almost [imitate] April perfectly&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;<q>Now, Girl! Dude! Just come and do it!</q> And I said, <q>Okay; but I’ll just come and do it temporarily,</q> and that was three years ago. And, so, I guess I’m in&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;and actually, […] we talk a lot about the band and it’s become a big focus of my life. And I love it! I lover her&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I’m not only glad that the band’s gone where it’s gone, but I’m glad that my friendship with her has taken on a new level, and I’m really freakin’ happy that I have friends in this band […]. These are my best friends.

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-05-13-chloes-kamps/czahller-20121201-23-03-37.jpg", "", "Brandie Dawson plays a Fender bass. Her eyes are closed. She has long, straight, dark hair and wears a leather jacket.", "Brandie (I)", "large" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

We really don’t fight; I mean, we fight, but it’s kinda ridic-&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I mean, we fought last week&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;[*Chapman’s husband, <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Kevin</span></span>, imitates cat screech*]&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;to be perfectly honest; and then, immediately&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Kevin Chapman</span> Your cat fights are ridiculously minor. *We’ve* been in bands together [*indicates himself and Lysandra*], and our fights were astronomical.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Exactly. We were in a band together, and we had to quit the band, because we almost got a divorce! ’cause I would say, <q>Well, you’re rehearsing on the side; well what is going on?</q> It was horrible!

<span class="spkr">K. Chapman</span> Well, because I kept saying <q>Darker!</q>

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Yeah, <q>darker,</q> what does that mean? And it’s not that I was opposed to that; it’s that there’s something about bands like, I don’t know how bands like, well, <span class="h-card p-org">Sonic Youth</span> is not a great example any more, because they’re divorced, but you know, how [do] some bands do it?

<span class="spkr">K. Chapman</span> <span class="h-card p-org">Yo La Tengo</span>!

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Brandie and I got in a fight last week, and within ten minutes I wrote her and I said, <q>I’m not mad at you and I’m sorry I was a jerk.</q> And she wrote back [and said], <q>It’s all good and I love you.</q>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You told me earlier that everybody in the band has a rôle; let’s go ’round the circle and talk about that.

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-05-13-chloes-kamps/czahller-20121201-23-42-06.jpg", "", "Leila Wright smiles while she plays the drums. She wears a scoop-neck sleeveless singlet (tank-top) and a short, plaid skirt.", "Leila (VI)", "large" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Dawson</span> I’m responsible. I’m the one who says, <q>No! We should not do this!</q>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You handle a lot of the business end of the band.

<span class="spkr">Dawson</span> Yeah, I keep track of the merch money and paying the room rent for our rehearsal space.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Beyond that, you’re also the nurturer?

<span class="spkr">Dawson</span> Yes; absolutely.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> […] She’s very nurturing; she’s very solid; she’s very dependable. If I got too drunk to drive the RV&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;number one person, right here, even ’though she’s not listed [on the insurance policy]. She’s going to be the solid person, no matter what […], and you’ve got to have that in a band. I think a lot of bands fail because they don’t have that.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> So it came up earlier, Lysandra, that you are also the "mother" in the group, but in a different way from Brandie; you’re the disciplinarian?

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> I am disiplinary. I come from a different perspective, and, actually, April and I bonded over the [facts of] when we went to college, and what music influenced us, and because we talk on the phone every day for like, two hours about the band: Where we want the band to go, what we’re thinking. We come from […] a different place, musically, so I try to keep us within the confines of that identified track [that] we want to be on.

The funny thing is, April knows that track. April knows where she wants us to go, and I’m the enforcer. She is the more bendable, <q>Oh, yeah; we’ll try that,</q> but [then] she’ll say [to me], <q>Here’s what we need to do; we need to get back over *here*.</q> […] The great thing is, nobody really resists everybody’s influence; we all kinda take it as an attribute [of] the band. Like, Leila being the&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I mean, she really is the hippie-peace-and-love and relax, and let’s-enjoy-the-time&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-05-13-chloes-kamps/czahller-20121201-23-38-24.jpg", "", "Lysandra Chapman stands behind a keyboard. Her red hair is pinned up. She sings into a microphone, which she cradles in one hand. She wears a leather jacket.", "Lysandra (IV)", "large" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> I love all this crazy shit; I love foam swords and piñata heads and I love the toys and the flash and making a joyful experience&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> [*speaking to Wright*]&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;you love current things; and you’re really into current stuff and what people love [now]; there is a […] generation gap.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Leila and Tiffany, you’re both a bit younger than the other band members, right? five or ten years, something like that?

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Yeah, something like that. [*laughter*] But they are, and that’s a different generation. She […]&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;with her generational knowledge, [Leila] keeps us young.

So there is *some* resistance, because we know where we want to go, we know the sound we want to have, so we’re sometimes like, <q>Are we gonna do this? Are we gonna go this youthful-inspiration [direction]?</q> But, thank God we have that, because sometimes she’s so freakin’ right on.

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> It’s a balance. […] You take the new and the old and there’s an awesome middle ground: You go too new, and it’s like you’re a "flash in the pan"; you go too old, and then you’re irrelevant […].

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> […] And then Tiffany came in, and it was nice to have another young person in the band (even ’though she’s not that much younger, for the record). [*laughter*]

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> She *is* the baby.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> She is the baby. And Leila really loves electronic music; she’s very much into fun and cool stuff going on in Dallas with [<span class="h-card p-org">Ishi</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/ishi/id201708863?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and <span class="h-card p-org">Zhora</span> and stuff like that.

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> I’m obsessed with&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I just love all that shit. If you want the list of cool Dallas things, I can send that to you!

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Obviously, April and I are influenced by&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I mean, we love <span class="h-card p-org">[Pavement</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/pavement/id2988588?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}; we love [<span class="h-card p-org">Sonic Youth</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/sonic-youth/id36034?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}; we love [<span class="h-card p-name">Liz Phair</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/liz-phair/id22707?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. And we love new stuff; we love [the] [<span class="h-card p-org">xx</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/the-xx/id315473044?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and, things that are electronic&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I’m just gonna interject and say that I’m [*redacted*] years old, and even Pavement qualifies as new for me. I own two or three Sonic Youth albums, but a Pavement song could come on the radio right now and I wouldn’t know who the heck it was.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> If you had any idea&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;you just made me feel *so* youthful!

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Tiffany, we know you’re the quiet one and the shy one. Beside that, what’s your part in the band besides thrashin’ guitar player?

<span class="spkr">Tiffany Byrd</span> [I’m] laid back; down for whatever, kind of like a child: I don’t have any responsibility&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;[*laughter*]

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Yeah, you only have to get out there and *am-a-a-a-aze* the audience!

<span class="spkr">Byrd</span> I mean, I show up, and I can do stuff, and I’m pretty quiet until I get drunk, and then I’m the obnoxious one, like, <q>Wake up, man! Let’s party!</q>

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> She’s not shy; she does observe a lot. But, get a couple of vodka cranberries in there …

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> I think that people should watch out, ’though, because I don’t think we’ve had nearly enough of Tiffany. I think, as Tiffany gets more comfortable with the band, I think that it’s going to …

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-05-13-chloes-kamps/czahller-20121201-23-04-19.jpg", "", "Tiffany Bird plays electrick guitar. She wears a trilby hat. Her eyes are outlined with thick, dark eyeliner.", "Tiffany (III)", "large" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Byrd</span> It comes out a little more with each show. We played my first show after, like, a week of practice.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> We had a year; she had a week.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> That’s right; you told me earlier about rehearsing for a year before your inaugural show, which goes back to what you told me about how April always wants to do things right.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Yes, actually, we’re very much on the same line with that: The way we’ve handled our careers, the way she wants to&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;first of all, she always wants to be the best, so anybody [who] wants to argue with us about trying to become successful, it’s not so much that we’re&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;it’s not a *phony* desire; *it is our nature*. We *will* do this; we *will* get to this point, because we will always be searching for more, and we can’t stop it; it’s who we are.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> It seems to me that the bands who become successful, but then lose that success, are bands that didn’t have that attitude. The only ones who seem to stick around are the ones who really, *really* want it.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Right; I think it’s a personality type; I mean, we’re just not complacent. We have a good audience; our craft is […] out there on vinyl; it will *never* be enough. Who converses two hours a day about [their] band?

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You and April.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Me and April! Every day: <q>Hey, girl; what’s up?</q> Because we want *everybody* to hear it; that’s it.

## Recipe: The Chloës Martini

A riff on the Hoffman House, a martini with orange bitters. The addition of absinthe adds a subtle bitterness and a touch of anise.

### Ingredients

3&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&ndash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;4 drops
: Kübler absinthe blanc

1 dash
: Regan’s Orange Bitters

1 ounce
: Dolin dry vermouth

2 ounces
: Plymouth gin

### Garnish

1
: Long, thin, spiral strip of lemon peel

### Instructions

1. Rinse a chilled cocktail glass (coupé, Nick & Nora, or stemmed V-shape) with the absinthe. Discard excess
2. Combine remaining ingredients in a mixing glass with 2 small ice cubes and stir for 30 seconds, until well-chilled and slightly diluted
3. Strain cocktail into the prepared glass
4. Gently twist the lemon peel over the drink and drape it over the glass
5. Put that flute away & sip slowly enough to savor, but not so slowly that the drink loses its chill
6. Repeat as necessary
