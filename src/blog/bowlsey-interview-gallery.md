{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-02-28-bowlsey/czahller-20130208-21-05-09.jpg", "img-constrained", "Cid & Shraz singing & rapping a duet. In the background, a colorful graffiti-style mural is visible.", "Duet (I)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

Acoustic duo <span class="h-card p-org">Bowlsey</span> is moving fast. They granted <span class="h-card p-org">Mercury Photo Bureau</span> an interview before their second public performance, and in the intervening weeks became the second interview subjects for <span class="h-card p-org">REV HiFi</span> before joining a contingent of Oklahoma bands featured at this year’s <span class="h-card p-org">SXSW</span>. This bears repeating: they went from their first public performance to joining the <span class="h-event">Buffalo Lounge SXSW Oklahoma Remix Showcase</span> in less than two months.{.drop-cap}

Singer-songwriter <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Clarissa</span> <span class="p-nickname">“Cid”</span> <span class="p-family-name">Castillo</span></span> (a.k.a. “<span class="h-card p-nickname">Cidward</span>”) and rapper-songwriter, guitarist and occasional organist <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Taylor</span> <span class="p-nickname">“Shraz”</span> <span class="p-family-name">Mercier</span></span> settled on the sofa in the middle of the Plaza District’s **Velvet Monkey Salon** for a frank discussion of their past and present and their dreams for the future. They were later joined by their manager, <span class="h-card p-name">Justin Hogan</span>.[^1] Afterward, they entertained the music lovers gathered to hear them (while simultaneously confusing and delighting the late night shoppers coming out of the vintage furnishings and clothing store located in the salon’s rear).

[^1]: Since this article posted, Hogan has become a full-fledged band member. Also joining as full-time drummer is <span class="h-card p-name">[Donald Eisenberg](/blog/psychic-milk-hilo-club/)</span>.

<span class="spkr">Mercury Photo Bureau</span> Introduce yourselves.

<span class="spkr">Taylor “Shraz” Mercier</span> We are Bowlsey. I’m Taylor, also know as Shraz.

<span class="spkr">Clarissa “Cid” Castillo</span> And I’m Clarissa, also known as Cid.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Why Cid?

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> Well, *he* [*gesturing at Mercier*] kind of named me that, because me and all my sisters have [nicknames inspired by our different attributes. My nickname is from <span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">Sid Vicious</span>] because of all the rotten things I used to do.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> How did you meet each other?

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> Well, we just bumped into each other in high school; [we] had never seen each other at the school until […] the end of the year. [We] bumped into each other, couldn’t tear ourselves apart […], but right before&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I mean, we’d just met each other, and I was about to go back to Oklahoma [and] stay there for three months, and […] I wasn’t sure I was gonna be coming back.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Was high school in Texas?

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> High school was&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;first half in Oklahoma, second half in Texas. [I] started college in Texas, [then] withdrew and came back to Oklahoma, and Cid followed me here.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Tell me about your musical backgrounds.

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> The first interesting music for me was <a href="https://mercury.photo/trout-fishing-in-america-childrens-matinee-woody-guthrie-folk-festival/">[<span class="h-card p-org">Trout Fishing in America</span>](/blog/woodyfest-2013-trout-fishing/). […] That was my band, as a kid, and, outside of that, <span class="h-card p-name">[Colin Raye](https://music.apple.com/us/album/when-you-say-your-prayer/id258486665?i=258486671&amp;uo=4&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span>, <span class="h-card p-name">[Garth Brooks](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/garth-brooks/id329451?uo=4&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span>, <span class="h-card p-name">[Jimmy Buffett](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/jimmy-buffett/id61232?uo=4&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</span>; just a whole lot of that from my grandparents, ’cause I was raised by them.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You like songwriters who tell stories.

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> That’s actually very true. That’s interesting.

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-02-28-bowlsey/czahller-20130208-20-46-03.jpg", "img-constrained", "Cid smiling at Shraz during their Velvet Monkey show.", "Smile!" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> That eventually led you to being a musician?

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> My grandmother put me in piano classes when I was in kindergarten. I had lessons for about seven or eight years […]. After that&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;that was kind of the first spark I had that I [didn’t] want to keep regurgitating the same things from recitals […]. I had a whole lot more ideas than what [I was] learning in these little classes […]. So I said, <q>Why don’t I write some of my own things,</q> and I ended up coming up with [longer pieces that were] a whole lot more thought provoking than anything I was playing at the time.

That led to guitar, led to bass, led to electronic music, led to everything else. I did have [a few] guitar lessons, [plus] a little bit with bass, but after that everything was self-taught.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Did you research on the internet, like watching YouTube tutorials?

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> Not really; I got enough tips here and there, more like&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I was just looking for a kind of feel, looking for where my little niche was with my instrument; how to feel out my instrument, and just find my [musical] groove […]. That’s where I’ve spent the past couple of years; I haven’t really listened much to the radio. [*laughs*] I’ve just been focusing on the actual&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;like, <q>Where do I lie?</q>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Apart from radio, do you listen to much music?

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> I haven’t for a while. My father, Patrick Mercier, was someone who always listened to music; the whole entire&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;any time you were in the car, any time you were around him, there was always music going. I appreciate that; I love music, but I just had to take a while&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;of silence, and, in that, I started writing more and more of my own music. It just put me more into my own thoughts.

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-02-28-bowlsey/czahller-20130208-21-05-48.jpg", "img-constrained", "Cid & Shraz singing and rapping.", "We Are Bowlsey (V)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> My mom and dad were runaways when they met each other; my father is aboriginal Australian, and he was a […] punk kid when my mom met him. My mom was in a [Houston] gang&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;she was a <span class="foreign" lang="es">chola</span>. My grandmother on my dad’s side is full-blooded aborigine; my [paternal] grandfather is Australian; he took her and kinda “smuggled” her over to the U.S., and she acclimated to Louisiana. [My father] was mostly raised in Houston; [he was] a bit of a mud dog [and did] construction.

And, he listened to a lot of rock; a lot of <span class="h-card p-org">Megadeath</span>, and [<span class="h-card p-org">Aerosmith</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/aerosmith/id115386?uo=4&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, and a lot of [<span class="h-card p-org">White Zombie</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/white-zombie/id115954?uo=4&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and [<span class="h-card p-name">Rob Zombie</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/rob-zombie/id110374?uo=4&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. And my mom [listened to] [<span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">South Park Mexican</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/south-park-mexican/id61750069?uo=4&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and [<span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">Notorious B.I.G.</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/the-notorious-b.i.g./id5499810?uo=4&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, [<span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">Tupac</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/tupac/id3502678?uo=4&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}; a lot of Houston rappers, too.

And I started orchestra [playing] viola. I stuck to viola, and I submerged myself in orchestra […] until senior year, when I really started listening to [Shraz’s] music, and we really started talking more. We were *engaged* by that time.

[…] I used to sing punk music a lot with my friends&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;it’s so terrible; I was in high school&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Could you give me an example?

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> When you … hear … stuff that I used to listen to; like [<span class="h-card p-org">Jack Off Jill</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/jack-off-jill/id167015294?uo=4&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, [<span class="h-card p-org">Scarling</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/scarling/id5678221?uo=4&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, [<span class="h-card p-org">Mindless Self Indulgence</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/mindless-self-indulgence/id774261?uo=4&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, stuff like that.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You seem to be ashamed of that. Why?

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> Because, there’s so much more. I kinda [realized that] after [Shraz and I] started talking to each other, and [I was living at a friend’s house] and [Shraz] ended up just staying with me all the time. And he was listening, and he said, <q>You could do better; you could do a lot better.</q>

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> I always tell you, not that you can do better; everything was there; you were just delivering it wrong.

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> Yeah; it made sense with the song you’re gonna hear during the show; it’s called <cite class="short-work">Extracurricular,</cite> and it was *foul*. Even though it’s the same lyrics, the way it’s delivered, the feelings that were sent through it weren’t the same.

I gotta say that Shraz made me grow up [musically]; after a while, I started listening a lot to acoustic [music], and I started to understand more about vocals and delivery and lyrics.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> When did you decide to perform in public?

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> Technically? Last month. [*laughter*]

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-02-28-bowlsey/czahller-20130208-20-35-01.jpg", "img-constrained", "Justin Hogan playing the electric organ.", "Justin Plays Little Kimmie (I)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Well, how did it happen?

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> It’s just been something we’ve been leading up to. We had our own musical backgrounds, completely separate; we didn’t listen to any of the same things growing up; then we became musicians, developed our own music, and *then* [we met] each other. Grew up&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> When you say <q>grew up</q> how old are you?

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> I’m twenty-one.

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> And I’m twenty. He met me at fifteen, and I raised myself. So I was basically just a walking nightmare.

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> There’s been a whole lot of growth between us […].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> How did you meet [Justin Hogan](/blog/psychic-milk-hilo-club/)?

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> After we moved to Oklahoma, we were living with my grandmother. We got kicked out of there; Cid was working at an apartment complex at the time, and she […] got us a good deal on an apartment. Six months later, we were helping a friend clean up a house he lived in, and I looked around, and I said, <q>You know, I never really looked at how nice of a house you have; I’ve been here tons of times, but, it’s a nice house!</q> I mean, especially after spending eight hours cleaning an oven, [I had a lot of time to observe the house].

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> Yeah, I was a renovation maid, which means you go into houses […] and you make it look like [nobody’s lived there].

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> We basically did that for this house, and after we discovered the house that was actually under [the grime], we asked [our friend] what was the price […]. And it was […] five dollars less than [our apartment rent]. And we’d be getting an extra bedroom […].

[Justin wasn’t living next door then], but he was there [by the time] we moved in, because he was house- and dog-sitting. He’d hear us jamming, and he found it really interesting, and he was like, <q>You really need to show people this; I know that you think that you’re just goofing around, but this is actually worth sharing.</q>

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-02-28-bowlsey/czahller-20130208-20-53-50.jpg", "img-constrained", "Shraz singing and playing acoustic guitar.", "Shraz (VII)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> He did a [school] project about us.[^2] [It was] a little slideshow about us, recorded on his phone, performing a song [while we were sitting] on the couch, and the [other] students […] loved it. He calls us and says, <q>Hey, you guys want to come to ACM[^3] and play for some kids in a class?</q>

[^2]: Hogan attends classes at the Academy of Contemporary Music at the University of Central Oklahoma.

[^3]: The Academy of Contemporary Music.

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> It was an artist’s development class. [There were about ten students and the teacher, Dr. <span class="h-card p-name">Trey Rick</sppan>].

After we were done playing, they critiqued us. Even though I don’t think we had done our best, everyone just kept giving us positive, positive, positive, positive; you know, wanting to see more, even the tiniest little bits we could give them.

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> Yeah, we only played three songs, just guitar and voice. We couldn’t even bring **Kimmie** because it was raining.[^4]

[^4]: Kimmie is the larger of Bowlsey’s two Kimball organs. The smaller organ they brought to the Velvet Monkey is “Little Kimmie.”

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Okay, tell me about the organs.

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> The first one we got was when we were still living with my grandmother, and that one wouldn’t even fit in my car; this one [*gestures at* **Little Kimmie**] does. It’s a **Broadway**; the first one is a Kimball **Paradise**. That one is just 3-inches wider, [but it has] quite a few more features. But that one’s the house organ.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Are you attached to analog, or would you be just as happy playing a digital synth with an organ plugin?

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> I couldn’t do that. When you see it there&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;it’s part of the show. Some people have lasers; I have an organ.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Where does the band name come from?

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> Back in Texas, [whenever we were hanging out with friends,] everyone being mischievous, we got bowlsey. We didn’t get ballsy; we got bowlsey. And it was for all the little mischief, from toastin’ to boastin’. Of all the names I could have thought of, it’s not the one that means the most to me, but it’s the one that described what we were doing best […].

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> It’s how we explained our agenda for years.

[*Justin Hogan arrives at the venue; we exchange greetings*]

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Have you started a recording project?

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> We’ve been working on what we were doing for so long, we didn’t have anything but that focus; on top of that, we [didn’t have resources to record; we] just [had] the microphone that comes on your Mac. And so, after running into Hogan and him introducing us to everyone in the whole town&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-02-28-bowlsey/czahller-20130208-20-51-53.jpg", "img-constrained", "Cid stands, looking pensive behind the microphone, fingers interlocked.", "Cid (VII)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I see you’ve acquired some new equipment; is it on loan, or do you own it?

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> [*guestures at Hogan*]&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;He’s moved in&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You’re not the dog sitter anymore.

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> I took him in!

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> He’s officially a tenant.

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> Yup; we took him in and he brought all his recording stuff with him and said, <q>We’re gonna go to work!</q>

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> So, we’re just trying to [record] all the stuff that we have; we have everything *down*, but we just need to get it recorded […]. We have all the instrumental [tracks; we just need to record the vocals and other layers].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> How many finished songs have you written?

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> Finished? Between seven and ten.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Are there some unfinished ones that might end up on a release?

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> Oh, yeah. You gotta understand, me and Taylor had tons of stuff [written] before we met each other […]. I’m still picking up old notes and then coming up to Taylor, who’ll have these great little ditties on his guitar, and coming in and saying, <q>Wait! I have something!</q> and creating […] songs out of the blue.

<span class="spkr">Justin Hogan</span> What’s cool is, I could hit just one note on the keyboard, and [*snaps fingers*] [Cid] starts singing. I can hit one note on the keyboard, and we get a whole song out of it! It’s pretty amazing […].

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> Throughout my life […], I’ve always played [music] alone. I’ve never had anyone to play with. All my friends were musicians, but every time I asked, <q>Hey, guys! You wanna play?</q> [they’d answer] <q>Naah; we’re playing with *these guys*.</q>&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-02-28-bowlsey/czahller-20130208-18-37-14.jpg", "img-constrained", "Bowlsey’s Kimball organ.", "Little Kimmie" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Hogan</span> *Bastards!*

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> &mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;and no one ever jammed with me. So, when I finally [met] Hogan, he […] took the time to listen, [to help me] focus, [to] calm down a little bit, and then [he] jammed with me […].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> It’s time to play “redeem a song.” What’s a musical guilty pleasure of yours, and what qualities make it worthwhile?

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> *Ohhh, noooo!* I’ve gotta think for a little bit. Alright; this is terrible. This song, the only way that this song can mean anything to you, is if you are at your all time lowest […]. It’s <cite class="short-work">Riddla on da Roof,</cite> by [rapper] South Park Mexican. It’s one of those things that you may think is just silly; the lyrics are terrible, it’s just a repetitive song; but then you start thinking about this person who, he doesn’t have very much&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;he thinks he’s living a life, yet he’s living [that] life in one town; it’s the best thing that he’s ever gonna get, and to him it’s fantastic and he’s gonna die loving it&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;but in your head, you’re just stuck here in this rut in yourself, and it makes you sad, too.

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> When it comes to [music I like], I will tell everyone about it. But, I could say something that I do find admirable, that isn’t something that everyone [else] does. [It’s] by <span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">MF Doom</span>, the villain himself, for his [DANGERDOOM](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/dangerdoom/id151201682?uo=4&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}[^5] project, and he does one called <cite class="short-work">Vats of Urine.</cite>

[^5]: DANGERDOOM is a musical collaboration between DJ/producer <span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">Danger Mouse</span> (Brian Joseph Burton) and rapper/producer MF Doom (Dumile Daniel Thompson).

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> I *knew* you were gonna say that!

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> And the whole, entire rap is about piss. But, if you weren’t told the title […]&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;he just says "urine" at the beginning&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;you would just bob your head and get lost in it, lost in the music, and his flow is so crisp and clean that you forget […] that he’s talking about peeing.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Is there anything else you’d like to say?

<span class="spkr">Mercier</span> This is a brand thing for both of us, and there’s a lot to come, because we haven’t brought in the full aspect of what do, yet […].

<span class="spkr">Castillo</span> We’d like to remind people that this has been only over the course of a month.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Your heads must be spinning.
