In part two of our interview with the <span class="h-card p-org">Chloës</span>, the ladies talk about the joys and pitfalls of auditioning drummers, keyboardist <span class="h-card p-name">Lysandra Chapman</span>’s mind-control techniques, and the difficulty of breaking into the Dallas&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&ndash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;Fort Worth music scene. Plus, Chapman reveals how she came to speak Icelandic.

<span class="spkr">Mercury Photo Bureau</span> Tell me about how you auditioned drummers before you chose <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Leila</span> [<span class="p-family-name">Wright</span>]</span>?

<span class="spkr">Lysandra Chapman</span> So we lost our drummer; she was great&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;she had written a song, it was so great. And I will say, it was very disappointing. She was in a relationship, and it went bad, and she had to leave. And it was one of those very immediate [situations]; it wasn’t like <q>I’m giving you advance notice.</q> And we were about to the point where we could play [in public], we thought. We were really close. But that definitely put a damper on things.

So [auditions were] probably a six-week process; we started going on <span class="h-card p-org">Craigslist</span>, and we got a girl first, and, there is a certain&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;this sounds very petty, but we have a certain look in this band, and you kinda have to fit that look in *some* way. I mean, obviously, we all look *very* different, but she was very typical of girl rock/punk bands. [*speaking to <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Brandie</span> <span class="p-family-name">Dawson</span></span>*] You might be better able to describe it.

<span class="spkr">Brandie Dawson</span> She was quite a bit older than us.

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-05-14-chloes-opolis/czahller-20130414-07-07-54.jpg", "img-constrained", "Lysandra Chapman. Only her head is visible above her keyboard.", "Lysandra (I)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> I think she might have done pretty well in, like, a "rage" girl&ndash;punk band. She was probably nearing fifty? And it sucked, because that’s not what we ascribe to. We don’t want to, in any way, discourage *any* woman, but you have to look at what we’re trying to put across, and there is some sort of image we were trying for. And that was very hard on us; [we want to promote] *all* women doing rock. […] It was good, but it just didn’t feel right. […] We *did* keep her on the shortlist.

<span class="spkr">Dawson</span> We had another girl come in who didn’t even have a drum set.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> She asked us to provide a drum set.

<span class="spkr">Dawson</span> It was like, <q>Okay … ?</q>

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> [Then] we opened up [the auditions] to men. Because, we thought, <q>We don’t *hate* men; we’ll let a man in,</q> and we got a guy [who] was freaking phe*no*menal.

<span class="spkr">Dawson</span> He was a great drummer.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Oh, my *God*! Like, any band would love to have this guy, but the problem was […] he had Asperger’s [Syndrome];[^1] […] he had a handler. And [the handler] walked in, and he explained the situation: that he does *not* talk; that he would talk *for* him […].

[^1]: Aspberger’s Syndrome is no longer recognized by the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM), the internationally accepted manual on the diagnosis and treatment of mental disorders. It has been merged with other conditions into autism spectrum disorder.

However, I have to say this; this is really funny: [the handler] was *so* nice. He was gonna help us load out; like he’d be participatory in the band, like he would be a helping hand, even ’though he was [the drummer’s] handler. So I thought that was really sweet. Like, he was really willing to get this guy [work], and I actually think that, for some bands that are in a certain niche, like some crazy "math-rock" band, I think [he] would work really well. For us, it probably wasn’t our scene. I wish we’d had the forethought to go, <q>Not for us, but this *crazy* band over here: you would really work really well in […].</q>

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-05-14-chloes-opolis/czahller-20130414-07-15-04.jpg", "img-constrained", "April Wenzel. She wears a sequined dress. Her arms are raised on either side; palm facing not quite forward, fingers extended.", "April (II)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

[…] That was probably our low point; we didn’t know *what* we were gonna do. […] And this guy at our rehearsal space said, <q>You gotta check out this girl, Leila. She had a band in high school, and she’s been playing with some guys. […] She’s on this tour of Europe, backpacking.</q> And I’m like, <q>Oh-my-god! Is she gonna be like this, total granola girl? Is she gonna be&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;</q> […] And she walks in, and she’s this blond bombshell. And [she] has her own gear. And sets up. And like, <q>That’s it!</q> Every song we had, I’m like, <q>This girl gets it.</q> I mean, it was right on, man! We didn’t even rehearse; she had it! [We were so confident that she was the right choice, that] when she was trying to load up [after the audition], we said, <q>Just leave your gear!</q>

<span class="spkr">Leila Wright</span> […] I had been trying to […] get something together for so long, and […] I’d been trying as many avenues as I could, and I just wasn’t getting any feedback. I was part of this teachers’ group, and I sent out [an email] blast, <q>Hey, anybody want to start a band?</q> I got back one response that was like, <q>Well, I did choir in high school …</q> and I’m like, <q>Arrggh!</q>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> [*laughter*] Could have been interesting; could have been the next <span class="h-card p-org">Polyphonic Spree</span>! [*laughter continues*]

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> But then I went to&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;it’s like a random series of events; completely random, and out of my, like, comfort zone? […] That led to me meeting this guy, who was a friend of a friend of a friend, and when I got that Facebook message, it was like, <q>Hey, you wanna come practice with us for <span class="h-card p-org">Catching Chloë</span>?</q> I remember we were in Germany in this crazy, modern hotel and I was bouncing off the walls with my mom, <q>Look at this band Catching Chloë! That is such a cool name!</q> It was like a gift from the universe.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> She totally mentioned our old name!

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I didn’t know there was an old band name.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> […] We had Catching Chloë&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;[<span class="h-card p-name">April Wenzel</span>] hears me, I’m sure&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I did not like our band name.

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-05-14-chloes-opolis/czahller-20130414-07-44-49.jpg", "img-constrained", "Leila Wright smiles as she plays the drums. She wears an aquamarine dress with a wide, sequined belt.", "Leila (III)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> It was confusing. [Promoters and MCs] would say <q>Chasing Chloë,</q> <q>Counting Chloë,</q> …

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> It was so hard; at every gig we had, they said it wrong!

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> And [the name change] came about completely spontaneously; we were at the practice space when&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> She *says* it came about spontaneously, but it’s a funny thing&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;it’s totally not; it’s totally engineered.

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> *What&#8253;*

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> *Totally* engineered by me. She had&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> *Really&#8253; This is totally news to me!*

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> *Oh, my God*, I totally engineered that.

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> We were […] on this load up thing outside, and some guy walked by and was like, <q>Huh, who’re you with?</q>

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> No no no no; you’re missing it. You’re thinking all wrong; what happened is, you guys had gone out for sushi. […] For the first freakin’ time ever, I was in the space before you guys were. You guys had walked in, and I said, <q>Oh, look, it’s the Chloës.</q> It was the same night. […] And you said, <q>Oh; I like that.</q> And, I was thinkin’ the whole time&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> You minx!

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> I *know*. And I was sneaky. I didn’t think it would happen that quickly; I thought it would plant the seed. You instantly were like, <q>I really like that name,</q> and so, like, later on, we went out, and April’s like, <q>I think that’d be really good if we shorten that up,</q> and then that guy walked by&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> Had you communicated with April before this?

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Not at all. No, no, not at all. ’cause I’d been in [the rehearsal] space for, like, thirty minutes, playin’ around, and I was looking at our posters, and I was like […], <q>*How* will I plant the seed?</q> I’m not taking credit, because if nobody would’ve agreed to it, it never would have happened. […] But it worked, and then that guy said [what he said], and April said, <q>We’re the Chloës.</q> It was just fast&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;one night, yeah.

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> It’s much easier to remember. And […] there’s a secret back story to the name […]. This is my personal interpretation, after several martinis, but I stay true to this. It’s like that bad-ass part of yourself that comes out, ’cause people can’t always be bad-ass-*raahwr!* all the time, like *on*. It’s that moment when you’re *on* and you’re, like, *in* it; that’s when you’re like a Chloë.

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-05-14-chloes-opolis/czahller-20130414-07-21-04.jpg", "img-constrained", "Brandie Dawson grimaces as she plays the bass. She wears a black lace dress.", "Brandie (I)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> I *love that*, and I think, lately, I don’t know if I’ve told you guys, but I’ve had *two* people say this to me recently, that Chloës is so much like that movie, <cite>[Heathers]("https://itunes.apple.com/us/movie/heathers/id468418525?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite>. Like that’s such a token, like, ’90s name? Of like the, uh, white, bitchy, *girl*; you know, but there’s a power in that? And look, let’s be honest, I mean, we can’t pretend to not be white girls. I mean, we are who we are; we’re these caucasian women who are in a band together. And two people have said the same thing to me, that it denotes this kind of *cool* name that *had* some power.[^2] Someone who’s kind of a bit bitchy, but [also] a little bit powerful and cool, and was like, <q>I kinda like that.</q> […]

[^2]: At this point, local slam poet <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-nickname">Tapestry</span></span> joins us; during the ensuing conversation Chapman mentions that she and the other women met him through <span class="h-card p-name">[Chris Sanders](/blog/venditos-interview-gallery/)</span>.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> So, tell me how you met Chris Sanders.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Easy. Like, the first NMF we were at, the first Norman Music Festival, he played after us. And he called us, and he said, <q>We like you.</q> I think he might have Facebook messaged us […], and he reached out to April and said, <q>I want to have you guys play here more often.</q> And that was probably the first act of kindness that *any* other band gave us.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You told me earlier how hard it is for women’s bands to make it in the Dallas&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&ndash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;Fort Worth area.

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> That’s *any* bands. It’s not women; Dallas is a competitive scene. It’s a good scene, but it’s like, you do for your own, and it’s a *very* different vibe. We love playing in Oklahoma.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I’ve only been interviewing bands since last October, and I figured out really quickly that the bands in Norman and Oklahoma City all help each other.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Totally. They don’t do that in Dallas […]. If you get to a point that you can benefit each other, then it works. Someone will allow&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;<q>I will help you, because it will benefit me.</q> That’s never been a motivation for Chris, or from [<span class="h-card p-org">Brother Gruesome</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/brother-gruesome/id351259346?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;we’ve played with Brother Gruesome, and I’m pretty close to them; <span class="h-card p-name">Brian Cagle</span>, <span class="h-card p-org">The Planets Align</span>, the [<span class="h-card p-org">Venditos</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/the-venditos/id317690352?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, and <span class="h-card p-name">Kerry Myers</span>&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;she doesn’t even play music, but came in and said, <q>I really like you guys, and I want to help; and you’re not from here, but I don’t care!</q>&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;and [<span class="h-card p-org">Skating Polly</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/skating-polly/id433876026?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, who are going *gangbusters*, I mean, however, still really like us, and we really like *them*. And, sadly, let’s be honest; I’m the same age as <span class="h-card p-name">David Mayo</span>. [*laughter*]

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> That’s Kelli’s[^3] dad.

[^3]: <span class="h-card p-name">Kelli Mayo</span> writes, sings, and plays basitar (a modified bass), keys, drums, and guitar at [Skating Polly](/blog/skating-polly-interview-pt-1/).

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Right. And so we’re the same age. They were as supportive of us as we were of them, and it’s like, we don’t get that, by *any* stretch of the imagination, in Dallas. So this is our home, really; I mean, we joked, the other night, that we were gonna be a little bit "ghetto" and play in Dallas. […] But we get audiences here; we have fans who really like us, and at least take a chance on us and come out, you know? It’s very difficult, and the funny thing is, it’s hard to pay people back. Chris and <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Todd</span> [<span class="p-family-name">Fagin</span>]</span> and <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Levi</span> [<span class="p-family-name">Watson</span>]</span>[^4] want to come out, and it’s like, <q>We will give you a gig, but we can’t promise what you give us here.</q> And that’s just disappointing; that kinda weighs on my heart […].

[^4]: Fagin and Watson comprise Brother Gruesome.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I wouldn’t have met you guys, if it hadn’t been for Chris. I wouldn’t have met <span class="h-card p-org">Skating Polly</span> if it hadn’t been for Chris. And there’re probably several other musicians I wouldn’t know if it wasn’t for him. I think a lot of people owe that guy.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> That guy is top notch, man&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> Yeah, top notch&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> So, before the great recording fiasco[^5] [*laughter*], I had each of you talk about some of your favorite band songs. Maybe we could try it again?

[^5]: See [part one](/blog/chloes-interview-gallery-1/).

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> Okay, I already did my couple of songs, but I’ll step up for the&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;the name of the album, [<cite>Vanish</cite>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/vanish/id550353626?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, is the "umbrella" of how awesome and empowering that was. And I think, the premise was&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;so, you look at the album art, and it’s like this girl running towards the city, and on the back, there’s this guy on the bed and it’s got the open window? And I think all of these songs are like empowered of a female who *owns* her relationship [*turns to Chapman*]&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;*what are you whispering?*&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;you cut me off&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> No, I’m so sorry; I didn’t mean to be rude, but it sounds so much like <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-nickname">Madonna</span></span>; like, is this ’80s Madonna?

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Oh, in the background?

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> In the background; sorry; it was so weird&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;we were, like, watching you [*indicates Wright*] in […] your little heart-shaped <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Lolita</span></span> sunglasses, and Madonna is [playing] in the background, and it’s just like&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;anyway, it’s a dude, and it’s like vanishing, and&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Wright</span> The theme is like, do what you&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;make your relationships what you want them to be, and all of the songs are very relationship-based in how men and women interact with each other.

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-05-14-chloes-opolis/czahller-20130414-07-43-57.jpg", "img-constrained", "Tiffany Byrd leans over as she bends her guitar strings. She is smiling. She wears a black sleeveless jersey-knit top with an abstract cross design across the chest.", "Tiffany (III)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Tiffany Byrd</span> You know, dude, <cite class="short-work">[Nothing Lasts](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/nothing-lasts/id550353626?i=550353900&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite> is one of my favorite songs. I think it’s because I like the way it builds; like, it starts out slow, and then it just gets heavier and *heavier*, and by the end, April’s like, screaming hardcore&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I dunno, it just sounds good; it’s fun to play. As far as overall songs just to listen to, I would say <cite class="short-work">[Run Run Run](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/run-run-run/id550353626?i=550353809&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite>, because of the words, the meaning behind it all&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;[*a train goes past sounding its horn*&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;<abbr>Ed.</abbr>]&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;the one time I talk, the *train* comes? [*laughter*]

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> That song was inspired by Brandie, right? There’s the metaphor for what running represents, plus a literal part that’s about the act of running marathons.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> Which, if you knew us […], is a far-fetched notion! But, no, I mean you guys are actually physically fit. I’ll take that back to me.

When [April] sent me that track […], I could hear what she wants out of it, and it was the most beautiful song I’ve ever heard, and it really got more and more beautiful as it was built and grown. And I just said, <q>What is that about?</q> And I *literally* don’t listen to lines, so when you ask me about lyrics, I’m *terrible*. <q>Blewehewehweh!</q>

<span class="spkr">Dawson</span> Lysandra makes the sounds instead of singing the words.

<span class="spkr">Chapman</span> I make sounds; I speak that [<span lang="is">Sigur Rós</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/sigur-ros/id73720797?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} language. You know, it’s all about the feeling. And [April] said it started from Brandie doing these marathons, and she said it was such a beautiful notion that she was running, and what is she running for? And she said that then it translated over to *life*, and not just relationships, but life, and, <q>I can see the dirt, and I know that I’m on ground.</q> I still get goosebumps; it’s so spiritually moving, that if you’re in such a shit place in your life, there’s no distance you can’t recover from.[^6] I can step outside; I can put my foot on the ground; and at least I know I’m real. I mean, I don’t know if you guys have ever been there, but I’ve been there: where you […] feel so&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;like you’re nothing in this universe. But that notion of <q>You *are* something</q> because you can put your put your foot out, and you’re on the same ground as everybody else, so you have just as much opportunity. […]

## Gallery: The Chloës at <span class="small-caps">OPOLIS</span>

In mid-April, the Chloës donated their time and talent to a fundraiser for <span class="h-card p-name">Robb Hayes</span>, guitarist for Oklahoma punk rockers <span class="h-card p-org">Debris</span>. Local musicians <span class="h-card p-org">John Wayne’s Bitches</span> opened, while Tulsa favorites <span class="h-card p-org">Broncho</span> drew the show to its conclusion. For your enjoyment, we present the following pictures of sequins, sunglasses, and scorching rock ’n’ roll.
