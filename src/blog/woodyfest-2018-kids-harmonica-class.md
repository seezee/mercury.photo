The <span class="h-card p-org"><span class="p-name"><span class="p-nickname">Woody</span> Guthrie</span> Folk Festival</span> has provided a variety of children’s programming over the years. <time datetime="2018-07">Last year</time> the festival expanded from just organizing kids’ concerts to also hosting youth workshops.

On festival Friday, Woodyfest artists <span class="h-card p-name">Joe Baxter</span>[^1] and <span class="h-card p-name">John Williams</span>[^2] taught an introductory harmonica class at the <span class="h-card p-org">Okfuskee County Historical Society</span> located in downtown Okemah. A dozen children and their parents attended, seated in metal folding chairs or standing at the room’s perimeter while Baxter and Williams showed the proper harmonica grip. After the students mastered the grip, they were ready to learn “the locomotive,” an inhale-exhale exercise evocative of a train racing down the tracks.

[^1]: The Oklahoma songwriter is so well-respected that in <time datetime="2016">2016</time>, a group of fellow musicians got together to record <cite>Tribute to the Songs of Joe Baxter,</cite> a (now out-of-print) compilation featuring covers of songs that Baxter wrote from <time datetime="1992">1992</time>&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&ndash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;<time datetime="2016">2016</time>.

[^2]: When he’s not teaching kids to play harmonica, Williams sings and plays guitar and harmonica with <span class="h-card p-org">[Gypsy Twang](/blog/woodyfest-2018-gypsy-twang/)</span>.

By the time we ready to move to our next assignment the class had played their first melody. All they needed was a campfire and a pot of beans to complete the scene.
