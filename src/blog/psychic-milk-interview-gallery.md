# {{ title }}

<!-- markdownlint-enable MD025 --><mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2012-11-12-psychic-milk/czahller-20121013-22-11-14.jpg", "img-constrained", "Leon goes full “scream-o” mode at the HiLo Club, singing/screaming while playing electric guitar. The image is black-and-white.", "Leon (V)", "eager" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

We interviewed neo-psychedelic rockers <span class="h-card p-org">Psychic Milk</span> after after they opened for <span class="h-card p-org">[Kill the Reflection](/blog/kill-reflection-interview-gallery)</span> at Oklahoma City’s HiLo Club. Guitar players <span class="h-card p-name">Leon Manson</span> and <span class="h-card p-name">Justin Hogan</span>, bassist <span class="h-card p-name">Brandon Wilson</span>, and drummer <span class="h-card p-name">Charlie Marsel</span> drove down to the Bureau after classes&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;all of the band members attend <abbr title="University of Central Oklahoma">OCU</abbr>’s Academy of Contemporary Music (ACM)&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;to answer a few questions.

Also in this interview we feature an exclusive first listen to <cite class="short-work">Orion’s Destruction Buckle.</cite>

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2012-11-12-psychic-milk/czahller-20121013-22-04-04.jpg", "img-constrained", "Leon plays guitar while thrashing his head side-to-side. The energy of his movement forces his mouth open in a grimace and causes his long hair to fly outward.", "Leon (I)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Mercury Photo Bureau</span> Who started the band?

<span class="spkr">Leon Manson</span> Well, me and Charlie were living together […] and then we met Justin at ACM, and we were just kinda sittin’ around, and I’d just quit the band I was in, <span class="h-card p-org">Moon</span>, and I was kinda startin’ to write with <span class="h-card p-org">[SonicViolence](/blog/sonicviolence-interview-gallery)</span> […] and we kinda just all gathered ’round one day and asked, <q>Why the fuck aren’t we all in a band together?</q>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Were you all in bands?

<span class="spkr">Justin Hogan</span> I’d just gotten out of one. I was in a band called <span class="h-card p-org">Unmarked Cars</span>, and we’d just broken up. You meet people at ACM and you see who’s good and who’s got some brilliance in them, and you want to work with those people. Mutual admiration and them being cool people [brought us together].

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2012-11-12-psychic-milk/czahller-20121013-22-20-53.jpg", "img-constrained", "Brandons screams/sings while playing bass. His eyes are closed. The image is black-and-white.", "Brandon (II)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Brandon Wilson</span> I don’t think we could be in a band together, if we didn’t like each other.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Sounds like the stars lined up for you. Brandon, you have something to add?

<span class="spkr">Wilson</span> I don’t think it’s a coincidence that we started as <span class="h-card p-name">[David Goad](/blog/kali-ra-interview-pt-1)</span>’s backing band. I was fresh out of high school, and that’s how I met all of these guys, and eventually ended up playing with David.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Were all of you in band or orchestra in high school?

<span class="spkr">Hogan</span> I was in band for maybe, sixth through half of eighth grade. [I played] alto saxophone, but I got a cheesy little keyboard for Christmas when I was thirteen, and I was like, <q>Fuck saxophone, man; this thing is cool!</q>

<span class="spkr">Charlie Marsel</span> I was in percussion ensemble […]. I played vibraphone in jazz band, and drums, later. There was an age-based hierarchy. I was in jazz band for two years, and also in pep band. That was lame. [*laughs*] And also guitar ensemble, which is weird, ’cause I’m not very good on guitar.

<span class="spkr">Wilson</span> I had my fingers in all the pies. Choir, band from the middle of sixth grade through the end of high school.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Everybody in the band sings, right?

<span class="spkr">All</span>Yes.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Were you the kind of kid who went around the house singing all the time?

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2012-11-12-psychic-milk/czahller-20121013-22-22-04.jpg", "img-constrained", "Charlie stares straight ahead while he plays the drums. His posture is very straight. The image is black-and-white.", "Charlie (IV)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Marsel</span> Every little kid sings. Every one of us was a singer before, but none of us was singing in a band&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;most of us played instruments. Psychic Milk meant learning how to sing again.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Who writes the lyrics?

<span class="spkr">All</span>All of us.

<span class="spkr">Hogan</span> Depends on the song.

<span class="spkr">Manson</span> There are some songs with a verse by one person and a chorus by another person. Justin […] has written a lot of the lyrics.

<span class="spkr">Hogan</span> And it’s cool. I’ve been in situations where you’re in a group, and one person does that, and that’s their department. This is fun; it’s kind of “Beatles-ey” to me, just because everybody contributes […]

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Do you find yourselves having a song idea outside the group, maybe sketching it out a bit, before bringing it to the others?

<span class="spkr">Manson</span> Yeah. Somebody will be like, <q>Here’s this chord progression I have,</q> [and] we’ll kind of sit around together, jam it out […] until we have something we like.

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2012-11-12-psychic-milk/czahller-20121013-22-02-01.jpg", "img-constrained", "Justin concentrates on his guitar. His chin is down and his eyes are closed. The image is black-and-white.", "Justin (I)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Hogan</span>  It’s fun, because there’re times when everybody’ll bring something, and it just transforms into something completely different than what I thought of, or what you guys might have thought of […]. You give off something, and it echoes around everybody, and it comes back and it’s this better thing [that] I wouldn’t have thought to do in a million years […].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Do you guys have songwriting sessions? Where you’ll say (for instance), “we’re going to write three songs in a certain time span”?

<span class="spkr">Manson</span> We’ve done that a few times. It’s based on how we’re feeling; there’s not a whole lot that can be done chasing the muse; instead, I’ll call someone and say, <q>Hey, man, I’m in a writing mood. You want to get together and make some music?</q> Everybody has different schedules; there’ve been a few times when we got together and said, <q>Okay, everybody, we need a new song,</q> and we’ll write a new song, but it’s usually not in any thought out way; it’s usually happens spontaneously.

<span class="spkr">Hogan</span> It depends on the circumstances. <q>We’ve got this show, we’d better have some new material.</q> That’s how <cite class="short-work">Orion’s Destruction Buckle</cite> came about.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Tell me about <cite class="short-work">Orion’s Destruction Buckle.</cite>

<span class="spkr">Wilson</span> It was a [chord] progression I’d been mulling over for a while. I think it came from a school project I was working on; it was more-or-less sound design, rather than composition, but I kind of built it all up just to break all it down, electronically, just for the root idea. It was just three or four keyboard parts all sequenced and then [arranged for guitars and bass], and Charlie plays keyboard on that, as well […]. We all have other projects going on, where we think, <q>This could go with Psychic Milk, or it could go with [another project].</q>

<span class="spkr">Hogan</span> On another note, I’d like to mention that Brandon is our Chief Science Officer. He *is* wearing blue right now; he’s got his pin. This guy’s a genius.

[*Brief discussion with Brandon about sound designer and film editor Walter Murch omitted for brevity*&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;Ed.]

<span class="spkr">Marsel</span> What’s cool about the way <cite class="short-work">Orion</cite> came about is that Brandon brought in the embryo, the keyboard parts and the sequences, and I was inspired to extrapolate from that onto the drums. It’s actually fun to play, too.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> If you guys had to market yourselves, if an <abbr title="artists & repertoire">A&R</abbr> man were sitting in the room with you, and said, <q>We need to put you in a genre, so we can sell your music,</q> how would you describe the band?

<span class="spkr">Marsel</span> Didn’t we decide “comic book&ndash;core”?

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2012-11-12-psychic-milk/czahller-20121013-22-11-27.jpg", "img-constrained", "Leon bends down while he plays a solo. His hair drapes down nearly to the ground. The image is black-and-white.", "Leon (VI) (Thrashing)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Manson</span>  I really hate the term “psychedelic rock,” because it’s so played out, and it’s such a thing for bands from Oklahoma to be categorized with the <span class="h-card p-org">Flaming Lips</span>. When we talked to A&R guys [in the past], it’s like, <q>Oh, psychedelic rock, Oklahoma, Flaming Lips,</q> that jump happens so quickly, I hate to even throw that out there, but it’s kind of the only thing that works. I like to think of it as just rock & roll music […]. It’s like if <span class="h-card p-name">Brian Eno</span> and <span class="h-card p-name">David Bowie</span> had some weird, fucked up space-child, and then that kid grew up listening to nothing but the <span class="h-card p-org">Who</span> and <span class="h-card p-org">Dinosaur Jr</span>. And <span class="h-card p-org">Black Sabbath</span>.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> That works for me. When you started with Eno and Bowie, well, they actually *did* what you describe [when they collaborated on the <cite>Berlin</cite> trilogy], but when you throw those other elements in, it makes perfect sense.

<span class="spkr">Hogan</span> Well, what did you think?

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I was having a hard time pegging it, figuring out who your influences were. It was a lot easier, Justin, when you did your second set with David Goad; the visuals are a pretty good clue there. He clearly takes his look from [<span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">Ziggy Stardust</span>&dash;era] Bowie and [<span class="h-card p-org">Bauhaus</span>’s] <span class="h-card p-name">Peter Murphy</span>. As far as your sound goes, I was having a hard time figuring out <q>Who do these guys listen to?</q>

<span class="spkr">Hogan</span> I’m proud of that. I like that.

<span class="spkr">Marsel</span> We all have such different influences, that also overlap in different ways for each person, that when it all comes together&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I mean, we’re all proud of our influences, and we’re wearing them on our sleeves in a way, but at the same time, altogether&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2012-11-12-psychic-milk/czahller-20121013-22-13-21.jpg", "img-constrained", "Justin screams/sings into the microphone. His eyes are closed. He wears a stingy-brim trilby hat. The image is black-and-white.", "Justin (IV)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Hogan</span> It’s different than with Goad, because then it’s *his* vision, it’s *his* direction, but with this, it’s every direction. It’s like a hadron collider.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> With <span class="h-card p-org">Kali Ra</span>,[^1] it’s obvious that the front man is the driving force, that the music is the product of a singular imagination, with you guys providing support. I really got the feeling watching you guys that I was hearing a collaboration.

[^1]: David Goad’s main musical project.

<span class="spkr">Manson</span> I didn’t know we *had* a front man.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You don’t, really. You have a singer, who also plays guitar; a different thing altogether.

Moving on, I’d like you to pick a song and redeem it. By that, I mean: Choose a song that is usually not perceived as very good, even though it might be hugely popular or successful, but that you might not want to admit that you like, and tell me why it’s actually a good song.

<span class="spkr">Manson</span> <cite class="short-work">[She Bangs](https://music.apple.com/us/album/she-bangs-spanish-version/id209710732?i=209711619&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"},</cite> <span class="h-card p-name">Ricky Martin.</span>

<span class="spkr">Marsel</span> *What‽* [*laughs*]

<span class="spkr">Manson</span> I have this weird relationship with pop music, where I will listen to Top 40 radio, and like, listen to <span class="h-card p-org">One Direction</span> or something, and just be like, <q>Oh my god, these chord progressions; they’re so fucking epic,</q> because there’s like, some motherfucker sitting in a studio trying to figure out [what the the best hook is]. I really, really, *really* love pop music for that.

<span class="spkr">Hogan</span> Like he says, you hear the chord progressions, and that’s just a particular reflection of those progressions through that medium; it’s like when <span class="h-card p-name">José González</span> did [his acoustic cover of <cite class="short-work">Heartbeats</cite>].[^2]

[^2]: <cite class="short-work">Heartbeats</cite> is a song by Swedish electronic musicians <span class="h-card p-org">The Knife</span>.

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2012-11-12-psychic-milk/czahller-20121013-22-05-44.jpg", "img-constrained", "Charlie holds both drumsticks, crossing one another, in his right hand above his head. He has just finished playing a complicated riff. The image is black-and-white.", "Charlie (III)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Marsel</span>  I grew up listening to a lot of 60s pop-rock; I really dig the accessibility of it. I understand a lot of modern pop, but it’s hard for me to relate to it. I’ve been drawn to, mainly, Scandinavian bands, being straight pop, but I can’t understand it, so it’s just sounds I can relate to, like another instrument.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I know I’m guilty of listening to a song, and, if there’s a really good hook, all I hear is the melody&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I might not hear the lyrics till the sixth or seventh time through. As lyrics writers, does that frustrate you?

<span class="spkr">Marsel</span> Different people listen their own way. I was talking to my mom yesterday, and she’s never been able to hear pitch; she can’t sing; which is why she really appreciates the words.

<span class="spkr">Hogan</span> I think lyrics are hugely important. Whether people listen to them or not, I have to be pleased with them.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Lyrics can be explicit, or they can be open to interpretation, provoking mood without a spelled-out narrative, like the difference between prose and poetry. Do you take a more prosaic or poetic approach to lyric-writing?

<span class="spkr">Manson</span> It’s generally stream of conscience when I write. As we’re playing a song, [I’ll improvise singing, and I’ll determine the meaning retrospectively]. I tend to mumble. It’s almost like with visual art, where you want people to have their own personal interpretation. I’ve always been the guy who messes up all the lyrics to every song; I’ll be singing, and people are like, <q>What are you singing? Those aren’t the words!</q> I like that; I want people to almost write in their own lyrics to what they think I’m saying, and draw their own picture, as opposed to <q>This is my vision, and these are my words! Hear them in your head, and know that it is true!</q>

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2012-11-12-psychic-milk/czahller-20121013-22-06-08.jpg", "img-constrained", "Brandon concentrates on a bassline, eyes closed, mouth agape. The image is black-and-white.", "Brandon (I)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Wilson</span>  I approach lyrics writing from a couplet-based, rhyming stanzas, formalist viewpoint. I’ve got, or I had, this weird obsession with symmetry and engineering.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> What’s a favorite song from your parents’ music collection?

<span class="spkr">Hogan</span> <cite class="short-work">[Dogs](https://geo.itunes.apple.com/us/album/dogs/1065974284?i=1065975105&amp;mt=1&amp;app=music&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"},</cite> by <span class="h-card p-org">Pink Floyd</span>; it’s actually from my <span class="h-card p-name">Aunt Deb</span>’s collection. Ever since I was thirteen years old, standing at the bus stop after Christmas break, and it’s like, really dark because it’s not daylight saving time yet, and I was freezing and I had this [Sony] Walkman, and I put this <cite>Animals</cite> tape in, and just the eeriness&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> That’s a pretty dark album. I would have thought that would be pretty scary for a little kid?

<span class="spkr">Hogan</span> Well I was listening to <span class="h-card p-org">KJ103</span>[^4] and <span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">Doctor Demento</span>, that was more my thing. Yeah, I discovered that, and that song in particular, especially <span class="h-card p-name">David Gilmour</span>’s solo. It totally changed my attitude [toward music].

[^4]: KJYO (known as “KJ103”) is a top 40 radio station serving the Oklahoma City metro. It is located at 102.7 on the FM radio band.

<span class="spkr">Wilson</span> For me, it’s either <cite class="short-work">Won’t Get Fooled Again</cite> or <cite class="short-work">Baba O’Reilly</cite> [from <cite>[Who’s Next](https://geo.itunes.apple.com/us/album/whos-next-deluxe-edition/974231776?mt=1&amp;app=music&amp;itscg=30200&amp;itsct=afftoolset_1&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite>] by the <span class="h-card p-org">Who</span>. My dad’s a big Who fan. I love the epicness, of form, of it being a comedy and a tragedy, with so many colors in a single song. Even more so with <cite class="short-work">Baba O’Reilly,</cite> because they pack so much into an even shorter song.

<span class="spkr">Marsel</span> <cite class="short-work">[Nowhere Man](https://music.apple.com/us/album/nowhere-man/id401134909?i=401135046&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"},</cite> by the <span class="h-card p-org">Beatles</span>. I was listening to the Beatles in the womb. Every day, I’d watch <cite>Yellow Submarine</cite>, and that was always my favorite scene, because of the character of Nowhere Man,[^3] and also because of that song, especially <span class="h-card p-name">George [Harrison]</span>’s solo: simple, but with a great presence. That got me into wanting to play music.

[^3]: The character’s name is actually Jeremy Hillary Boob, Phd.

<span class="spkr">Manson</span> When I was like, thirteen, I had a little record player, and my dad busted out his records, and the first one he gave to me, ’cause I had listened to a little Black Sabbath before, but he was just like, <q>Fuckin’ <cite>[Masters of Reality](https://geo.itunes.apple.com/us/album/master-of-reality/785239139?mt=1&app=music&itscg=30200&itsct=afftoolset_1&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite>, right here,</q> and I seriously sat there listening to <cite class="short-work">Sweet Leaf,</cite> like, twenty times, picking it up and starting it over, and I was like, <q>Holy shit!</q> So yeah, fuckin’ Sabbath!

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I’d like to thank you all for coming down.

<span class="spkr">All</span> [*various polite rejoinders*]
