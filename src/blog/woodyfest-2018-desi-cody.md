<time datetime="2018-07-13T15:00:00-5:00">Friday</time> at the festival</time> found this writer still covering the entertainment at Lou’s Rocky Road Tavern. [<span class="h-card p-name">Erik “<span class="p-nickname">The Viking</span>” Oftedahl</span> and <span class="h-card p-name">Blake Lankford</span>](/blog/woodyfest-2018-erik-the-viking-blake-lankford) had cleared the stage, making way for Tulsa duo <span class="h-card p-org">Desi & Cody</span>. Husband and wife <span class="h-card p-name">Cody Clinton</span> and <span class="h-card p-name">Desirae Roses-Clinton</span> entertained the audience with their sarcastically humorous indie rock, which <cite>No Depression</cite> describes as <q>[…] sun drenched harmonies and chamber pop textures [fused] into […] futuristic folk.</q>[^1]

[^1]: <cite class="h-card p-name cite-person">Small, Sloan</cite>, <cite class="short-work">Desi and Cody’s <cite>Yes, This Is Killing Us</cite> is Delightful Futuristic Folk,</cite> <cite>No Depression,</cite> (<time datetime="2018-10-19">19 October 2018</time>).

The pair met in college when Roses-Clinton heard a live performance of Clinton on the radio and resolved to see him perform in-person. They became friends that night and later started dating. The two shared a home by the time Clinton joined <span class="h-card p-name">Leon Russell</span>’s band.</p>

<time datetime="2009">Around that time</time> Clinton started recording his first solo EP and happened to hear Roses-Clinton singing in the shower. That’s amazing!</q> She was singing hardcore, super crazy high opera type stuff. I was like <q>Woah.</q>&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;</q>[^2]

## Misfortune Makes Music

Desi & Cody’s latest album, <cite>Yes, This Is Killing Us,</cite> is based on their experiences touring as a married couple. Clinton had previously contended with alcohol addiction but had been sober for nine years. But then the musicians each lost their fathers in a short time span. At the start of a European tour Clinton relapsed. They would spend the entire tour and much of the following year <q>just getting wasted.</q>[^3]

[^3]: <cite class="cite-person">Small</cite>.

The resulting collection is boisterous, darkly funny, mocking, and sometimes poignant, showcasing the pair’s strong storytelling chops. Besides the original material, Desi & Cody cover <span class="h-card p-name">Chris Isaak</span>’s <cite class="short-work">Wicked Games,</cite> albeit in an entirely novel arrangement.

Enough time and distance have passed for the humor to shine through&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;simultaneously fresh and nostalgic, the collection’s blend of <time datetime="1960">’60</time>s Motown and girl-group pop melded with touches of <time datetime="1980">’80</time>s new wave keep everything from collapsing into misery.

For their Woodyfest set, Desi & Cody were joined by drummer <span class="h-card p-name">Micah Moseby</span>, bassist <span class="h-card p-name">Tyler James</span>, and lap steel guitarist <span class="h-card p-name">Mike Kindell</span>.
