Comprising an ever-changing lineup of local musicians (including more drummers than <span class="h-card p-org">Spinal Tap</span>), <span class="h-card p-org">Venditos</span> have been rockin’ Oklahoma City for nearly a decade. Their sound is hard, heavy, and fast; their lyrics tend toward the funny and profane. Frontman and founder <span class="h-card p-name">Chris Sanders</span> and bassist <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Gabriel</span> <span class="p-nickname">“Gabe”</span> <span class="p-family-name">Barham</span></span> graciously granted the Bureau an interview in <time datetime="2013-02-07">early February</time>.{.drop-cap}

During a break from the interview, Sanders and Barham told us how the band name once almost got them killed. The band were playing at an Oklahoma City dive called <span class="h-card p-org">Spinozi’s</span>, which has since closed. The bar was a hangout for members of a local motorcycle club. When one of the motorcycle men asked Sanders his band’s name, he replied, <q>Venditos.</q> Unfortunately, the motorcycle club was the <span class="h-card p-org">Banditos</span>. In the noisy bar, motorcycle man misheard Sanders. <q>The Banditos? You can’t call yourselves that!</q> As the motorcycle gang menaced the band, Sanders nervously made a clarification, staving off disaster.

<span class="spkr">Mercury Photo Bureau</span> Please introduce yourselves.

<span class="spkr">Chris Sanders</span> I’m Chris Sanders; I play guitar and I sing.

<span class="spkr">Gabe Barham</span> My name’s Gabriel Barham, and I play bass and sing.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> And who’s not here tonight?

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> <span class="h-card p-name">Richie Zenner</span> is not here, and, for the show you saw at <span class="h-card p-org">Kamps</span>, <span class="h-card p-name">Austin Barham</span> was the drummer.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Do you currently have a drummer?

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> We’re gonna be working with several [drummers] on the album, and [I’ll probably] lay some of the drum parts myself. I have a few people in mind and I have contacted them, but I don’t want to say their names just yet [since nothing’s finalized].

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> We’ve pretty much been a revolving door on drummers since we started.

And, he really seems mad that I claim I’m from Apache, Oklahoma.[^2] I was actually born in Bowling Green, Kentucky, but [I grew up] in Apache [and] I graduated from [Apache High School]. So I claim that as my home town […].

[^2]: Located in rural southwest Oklahoma, Apache is home to wheat, cattle, and wind farms and an annual rattlesnake festival.

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> I have seen pictures of [Chris] wearing an Apache [High School] football uniform.

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> Any publicity’s good publicity, you know, like <span class="h-card p-name">Marilyn Manson</span>, shock rockers, shit like that.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> That goes back to <span class="h-card p-name">Bertold Brecht</span>, but if you’re going to invoke Manson, you’ve got to mention <span class="h-card p-name">Alice Cooper</span>, and, for that matter, <span class="h-card p-name p-family-name">Zappa</span>, whom I know you’ve cited as an influence.

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> Yeah.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I understand the band has its ultimate origins in the Oklahoma City open mic poetry scene. I know that you used to read at Galileo’s; did you also attend the Medina’s[^3] readings prior to that?

[^3]: Now defunct, Medina’s Coffee House in Oklahoma City’s historic Paseo District hosted poetry readings and open mic music beginning in the mid-1990s. Notable poets who got their start there include Scott Jonathan Nixon (“The [Czech] Presidential Poet”) and Beau Sia.

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> I did, and <span class="h-card p-org">Sandmans</span> [Coffee Shop] as well.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Okay, so I probably heard you read.

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> I was on a break [from a] band called <span class="h-card p-org">Closed</span>, and we had our short-lived [time]. It was from high school, it was, like, a seven&ndash;year long bill. We got a little bit of good publicity from it.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> When did you graduate?

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> <time datetime="2000">2000</time>. And then, after that band broke up, I really didn’t know what to do; I still wrote songs on acoustic [guitar], which most of them ended up being Venditos songs. I tried to go [to the open mic readings] at first and play acoustic guitar and sing […], and, I mean&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I like my songs; they’re just not interesting without a full band. I’m not an acoustic singer-songwriter kinda guy.

And then I started doing poetry; I [wrote] two chapbooks; I traveled around a bit with <span class="h-card p-name">Jonathan Bryant</span> and <span class="h-card p-name">Nathan Steinman</span>.[^4] It ended up being a [pretty serious] two- or three-year deal. But I always just loved playing music, and I started getting back into it. And every once in a while, I’ll pop in [to the poetry readings at Sauced on Paseo] and read.

[^4]: Venditos’s original bass player.

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> Probably both, because back [then], I did have hair […] to my shoulders, and I tried to grow a beard, which I still cannot do. But I just would not shave, and I can grow a goatee, so my goatee was, like, this long [*gestures to indicate length*], and then I had horrible patches&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> So, like a hillbilly goatee.

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> Like, <cite>Joe Dirt</cite>, ’cause I am just a queer from Kentucky. [*laughs maniacally*] I had to throw that in there!

I’m not actually gay; I’m gonna put that in there for the record.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> [*quoting <cite>Seinfeld</cite>*] <q>Not that there’s anything wrong with that!</q>

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> <q>Not that there’s anything wrong with that!</q>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Gabe, I know you came to the band later, because Chris is the only original member. How did you become their bass player?

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I could see him as Conrad Birdie.

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> Back then, he had hair down to his ass, and he was willing to cut it off [for the part]; that’s how much he wanted it. One day he brought his guitar up; we sat around playing on his acoustic, and he was like, <q>We should start a band!</q> Alright.

So I played in a band with him in high school; we were called <span class="h-card p-org">The Other Side</span>; we were horrible. If I had recordings to send you, I would, but he would stab me.[^5]

Stuff like that. I played with Spooky Fruit for a long time, and, as angsty, drunk punk rockers do, we got in a fight and we quit playing together.

Then I was in a band called <span class="h-card p-org">Old Major</span> for about another four years; got to play with a lot of cool bands; got to play with the <span class="h-card p-org">Subhumans</span> and Gwar and <a href="" target="_blank" rel="external me">[<span class="h-card p-org">Reverend Horton Heat</span>](https://www.flickr.com/photos/czahller/sets/72157631669370390/with/8044815890/){target=_blank rel="external noopener me"} and all that kind of stuff. And we got in a fight, and [then] I was like, <q>Screw music!</q> I got back into school, […] started college. [Around <time datetime="2006">2006</time>,] Justin calls me and says, <q>You still got all your stuff?</q> and I was like, <q>Yeah?</q> <q>Well, I know these guys; they need a bass player; you want to start playing again?</q> <q>Okay.</q>

So I went and met Chris […]; we decided it was cool. I faked my way through [some of their songs]; they said it sounded nice, well, okay, <q>Let’s do it; you’re in for now; we’ll see how it goes.</q> <q>Well, okay; when’s the first show?</q> <q>Oh, we go out of town Friday.</q> And this was on a Wednesday.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Did you have a day job then?

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> Well, I delivered pizzas and went to school. I had no problem with missing a couple days’ classes, ’cause I was on a pretty light schedule […].

I went and played with [Venditos] out of town; I think we played in Kansas for about three hours. All “hippie jam band” style. [We played three dates on that tour.] Ever since then, I’ve been the other stable [band] member.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Chris, give me the short version of the band origin story.

Me and him played with <span class="h-card p-name">Rob White</span> and several others and kind of made these recordings, and one day we got asked to play live, just at random ’cause I went to the HiLo [Club] a lot.

[…] We were drunken-ass buffoons; we sometimes would knock [people’s socks off] and other times [we’d] chase them out of the club [with] crazy, loud, noisy jam sessions.

Will ended up leaving […]; he wanted it to be a [recording project], and he’s doing that to this day […]. He has no desire to be in a band.

Nathan Steinman was one of the original [members], and he stuck with it for a long time […]; then we had a mishap.

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> Tell him the story.

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> We were on a mini-tour [to] Dallas, Austin, Baton Rouge, New Orleans, home. So, we make it all the way to New Orleans; he was there with his girlfriend at the time&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I don’t remember her name, but we called her <q><span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">Brother Clit</span>.</q> […] We went to a party after the second night, after we played the show one night; […] we had played a show the night before, but we were gonna stay there, hang out with some friends, and leave that night.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You wrote songs in pretty disparate styles for your first release, as opposed to the more cohesive sound of your current offering.

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> I’m [such] a 90s whore. That comes through in everything I write.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I know you list grunge as an influence, but you don’t sound like grunge to me.

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> I think we’re a lot cleaner.

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> I think we said that because it’s something we listended to a lot […]. The first time I saw Chris, he was in a godawful band that opened for <span class="h-card p-org">NOFX</span> […].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You’ve spoken elsewhere about how booze and drugs were a problem.

<div aria-hidden class="pullquote">

  We were drunken-ass buffoons; we sometimes would knock people’s socks off and other times we’d chase them out of the club with crazy, loud, noisy jam sessions.
</div>

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> I don’t think they were so much of a problem; we really loved it. But they were a problem with our ability to perform.

I’m thirty-one years old now […]; I love playing music; and I love gettin’ fucked up […]. But when I get on that stage these days, [even if you just] paid two dollars at the door, I want to give you what you fuckin’ paid for. I want to get up there and play a rock show […] and not be […] a drunken, fuckin’ mess playin’ [nine minute songs].

We used to push [the drinking] way too far, every time; it was a [status thing. Now, I’ll only] have a beer or two before I go on.

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-02-18-venditos/czahller-20121201-23-58-16.jpg", "img-constrained", "Chris screams into the microphone", "Chris (I)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> For me, it got to the point where I was telling the crowd that I was <span class="h-card p-name">Jesus</span> and yelling at them. I would finish off a bottle of Bushmills on stage, and that stuff just can’t happen.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You’ve had two tour buses?

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> [We bought the first bus] for $500. It was a 32-inch, sixty-six passenger International diesel schoolbus. I spent two to four years [customizing] it; it had Murphy beds, a makeshift bathroom, a kitchen, all our gear stored in the back. [It] got about nine miles to the gallon; at that time, diesel was seventy-nine cents [per gallon], so it wasn’t that big a deal. [Once you get a van and a trailer loaded down with gear and people, you’re getting similar mileage, anyway].

The next bus was […] an upgrade […]. It’s a 1972 MCI Greyhound […]. It was an evangelist’s bus.

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> Shag [carpet], floor to ceiling. It [still has] the Jesus [vanity] license plate: there are a lot of little towns in Texas, which is where we mostly play, when we’re out of town, that if they see a band bus, then it’s automatically pulled over […].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> When you played at <strong>Kamps</strong>, you played four songs you haven’t recorded yet:

* <cite class="short-work">Emo</cite>
* <cite class="short-work">Keeping Score</cite>
* <cite class="short-work">Happy Song</cite>

Plus a cover of Bowie’s <cite class="short-work">Life on Mars.</cite>

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> Like I said, the Vandals was a big [influence], and if you’ve ever listened to [them], they’re [only] about half serious […]. They’ve got a song, for instance, called <cite class="short-work">My Girlfriend’s Dead.</cite>

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> Is that the one that goes, [*singing*] <q>I invented socks/I invented gravy/I invented the cotton gin/but no one ever paid me</q>?

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> Yeah […].[^12] [The band] were talkin’ about emo kids one night, and I was like, <q>Dude, it’s not hard to write [emo].</q>

[^12]: Sanders’ quoted lyrics are actually a paraphrase of <cite class="short-work">Aging Orange.</cite>

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> We’d been making fun of, mainly, the vocals, you know, that <q>Nyeeah, nyah nyah!</q> [*imitates whiney vocals*]

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> I yelled something in my “emo” voice, and I think it was <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Kristine</span> [<span class="p-additional-name">Kamen</span> <span class="p-family-name">Wendt</span>]</span>, who’s our “band mom.” She [goes with us when we leave town, if she can, and makes sure] there’s healthy snacks on the bus; she really takes care of us. She’s a really sweet lady. And she was like, <q>You do that *way* too well.</q> […] So I just came up with a riff and put that on it.

[…] It was never meant to be a serious song, but every now and then, I’ll be walkin’ through a store, and I’ll hear [*whistles the chorus from* <cite class="short-work">Emo</cite>]. [Chris] even said his mom started humming it, one day, while they were drivin’ around.

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> [Regarding <cite class="short-work">Happy Song,</cite>] basically, I’m a nerd. […] a lot of my songs [have to do with] <q>Things are gonna get better; if you’re havin’ a bad time, don’t worry about it,</q> ’cause that’s what <cite class="short-work">BTFBTN</cite> is about […]. I’ve got some friends who have a lot of really bad shit goin’ on, and those songs are my way of makin’ them feel better […].

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> <cite class="short-work">Keeping Score</cite> is […] kind of a slower, pop-rock song.

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> Yeah, all “Hootie.”

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> It starts out all, <q>If you want to know all the facts of life, you gotta stand up and give it a try.</q> [It’s] part of some crazy […] concept album; it’s something that I’m in the course of writing. Gabe has some songs to contribute […]. We’ve talked […] about whether to release it as two EPs. It’s like a yin-yang thing[, with the two writers], but it’s in the works. So we really don’t know what’s gonna happen [with it].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Tell me about your current project.

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> So far, there’s thirteen tracks, all in various [stages of completion. They’re all still demos]. There’s no title yet.

I’m tryin’ to write somethin’ that’s […] following this [rock star character]; it’s a story that’s been told over and over […], but it’s about how ya tell it. It’s like a rise and fall […] thing. The thing is, we just got done spending a year doin’ [<span class="h-card p-org">Pink Floyd</span>’s] <cite>The Wall</cite>.[^13]

[^13]: Chris is referring to a fully produced tribute show of <cite>The Wall</cite> performed by a [collective](https://web.archive.org/web/20121105033347/https://www.okgazette.com/oklahoma/article-13504-great-%E2%80%98wall%E2%80%99.html){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} of Oklahoma City musicians in 2011.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Who writes most of the songs?

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> [Chris wrote] most of the <cite>Version 4.0</cite> stuff before we came into the fold. And then <cite>Secrets</cite> was a lot more collaborative.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Time to play “Redeem a Song.” What’s a musical guilty pleasure, a specific song you like, and why should I like it?

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> [*thinks for a few seconds, then:*] What’s that song, <q>I wish that you would step back from that edge</q>?

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> <span class="h-card p-org">Third Eye Blind</span>. [*Looks it up on smart phone*] <cite class="short-work">Jumper.</cite>

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> It’s a great fuckin’ pop song. The whole song’s a hook, from beginning to end. It’s shaped like a triangle; [it starts simple and small] and it builds.

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> I’ll just go with a whole band that I’m slightly embarrased that I like: <span class="h-card p-name">Dave Matthews</span> [Band]. I used to date a gal [who] would only have sex […] to Dave Matthews’s  double live [album], and that kind of endeared it to me. She was definitely the cutest girl that I’ve ever dated.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Thanks for coming down, and a shout out to your bandmates who couldn’t make it.

<span class="spkr">Barham</span> They’re gimps.

<span class="spkr">Sanders</span> The safe word is banana. [*laughter*]
