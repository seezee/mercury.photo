Local garage-rockers <span class="h-card p-org">Shitty/Awesome</span> (stylized Shi++ty/Awesome) formed on a whim in <time datetime="2009">2009</time>, when Guestroom Records co-owner <span class="h-card p-name">Travis Searle</span> and manager <span class="h-card p-name">Will Muir</span> got together with employees <span class="h-card p-name">Derek Lemke</span> and <span class="h-card p-name">Joey Powell</span> with the idea of a band that would only ever <q>play in the alley behind the store&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;or any alley, anywhere&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;or house shows.</q> They’ve since become local favorites, so metro music fans were saddened by the announcement last week that they would be playing their final show at this weekend’s <span class="small-caps">OPOLIS</span> birthday bash.{.drop-cap}

The reason for the breakup was explained in a post on the Guestroom Records [Facebook page](https://www.facebook.com/GuestroomRecords/posts/10151729485314367){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. The business is opening a new store in Louisville, Kentucky and closing the Bricktown, Oklahoma City location. Searle will move to Louisville to manage the new store, with Muir coming down to Norman to fill the vacancy created by the move.

Shi++ty/Awesome play their final show this <time datetime="2013-08-16T23:30-5:00">Friday at 11:30 p.m.</time> at <span class="small-caps">OPOLIS</span>, 113 N. Crawford Avenue in Norman, as part of the <span class="small-caps">OPOLIS</span> XI birthday [celebration](https://www.facebook.com/events/694448380569775/){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. The two-day event features sixteen bands, with free admission thanks the support of [Fowler VW](https://www.facebook.com/pages/Fowler-VW/117978531571775){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

Shi++ty/Awesome dropped by the bureau for drinks and conversation <time datetime="2013-08-12">this past Monday</time>, after their final rehearsal on <time datetime="2013-08-11">Sunday</time>.

<span class="spkr">Mercury Photo Bureau</span> Please introduce yourselves, describe your rôle in the band, and give me a short bio.

<span class="spkr">Travis Searle</span> Travis Searle; I play drums for Shi++ty/Awesome. Owner of Guestroom records and Norman … scenester? Soon to be moving to Louisville, Kentucky. [I’m] originally from Oklahoma City; [I] moved to Norman in 1999; went to school and … opened a record store in 2003, and it’s been … downhill from there. [*laughter*]

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-08-14-shitty-awesome/czahller-20121231-22-42-38.jpg", "img-constrained", "Travis Searle plays drums on New Year’s Eve. He wears a dark tank-top a white sweatband, the latter on his head.", "Travis (V)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Will Muir</span> Will Muir, and I play guitar and sing in Shi++ty/Awesome, and, I guess, musically, I’ve done a few tribute bands. I did a [<span class="h-card p-org">Smiths</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/the-smiths/id829538?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} tribute band, and Derek and I did a <a href="" target="itunes_store">[<span class="h-card p-org">New Order</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/new-order/id176722?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} tribute band together. I’ve lived in Norman for half my life, and that’s longer than I’ve lived anywhere. I moved here my *second* freshman year of college.

<span class="spkr">Derek Lemke</span> Derek Lemke; I play guitar for Shi++ty/Awesome; I’m the sexy one. [*laughter*] I was born in Norman, but I grew up in Noble, and I’ve been back in Norman since I was … eighteen? nineteen?

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> Oh, and not here is Joey Powell; he is our manager, and he’s the responsible one. [*laughter*] I’m just kidding; he *is* being responsible tonight. He’s working. *And* he plays bass and sings.

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> Originally the keyboard player. We started out as a three-piece, and we wouldn’t let Joey in the band […] for the first month or so, and then decided it would be okay if he played keyboard.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Was Joey already a bass player? or did he learn bass for the band?

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> No; he has been playing bass for half his life.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> So, why did you want him to play keyboard?

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> Uh, we just thought it would be funny. We didn’t even want a bass player; we wanted [the sound] to be very tinny.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> The band’s origin, basically as a joke, has been well-documented. How did you come up with the name?

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> [*Looking at* Derek *and* Will] Is the story I’m thinking of about the name&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> It was all you.

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> — it was […] at the beginning of […] garage rock’s resurgence, and we realized how much we liked bands that were, you know, kinda shitty bands, but you could still go out and have an awesome time, and it felt like a party; and, at the time, there wasn’t anything like that in Norman.

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> — and it was also pre&ndash;Guestroom Records Records&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;pre&ndash;the label; and we had talked about calling the label “Shitty/Awesome,” and having that as the descriptor for the music. But we dropped that, because we did know that wasn’t the best, uh, business decision. But, we felt like it described our band. It was better than <span class="h-card p-org"><span class="h-card p-name">Eric Clapton</span>’s Dead Babies</span>.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Do you have any regrets over the name’s scatological character? Has it ever caused you problems with promoters or news media?

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> You know, we never really tried; we never really cared&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> We have had some labels tell us it was a terrible name. But, they also loved the music.

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> I don’t think there have ever been any *serious* regrets; there are times where we look back on it and go, <q>Goddammit; if we would have had something that was … generic,</q> and whatever, the “somethings,” “The Toothbrushes,” or “The Pillows” or something like that, that maybe we could have had more of an impact outside of this scene; but also, maybe *not*? At least, this way, people saw the name and paid attention to it almost … almost immediately, because it was like, <q>Let’s see what kind of crap these assholes play,</q>&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> — *and* it’s funny […], but, it also&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;we haven’t been sticklers about, <q>Don’t call us anything else,</q> so people on the radio have called us about five or six different names. “S/A” is the one we tend to go by; “Shirty/Awesome” is one …

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> What was the first music you remember buying with your own money?

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> <span class="h-card p-name">Prince</span>, [<cite>1999</cite>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/1999/id214145240?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

<span class="spkr">Lemke</span> <span class="h-card p-name">Michael Jackson</span>’s [<cite>Bad</cite>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/bad/id559334659?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> I remember buying lots of stuff on my own; off the top of my head: <span class="h-card p-org">Beach Boys</span> and <span class="h-card p-org">Beatles</span> tapes, and late 80s “hair-metal” stuff.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> When was the first time you asked your parents to buy you a music recording?

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> I remember having to have my mom buy <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-nickname">Dr. Dre’s</span></span> [<cite>The Chronic</cite>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/dr.-dre/id35315?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, [but that was because I was underage]. But I already had over 200 CDs, albums, cassettes at that point.

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> It was the single for <cite class="short-work">[One Night in Bangkok](https://geo.itunes.apple.com/us/album/one-night-in-bangkok-single-version/24835137?i=24835176&amp;mt=1&amp;app=music&amp;at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite> from the musical <cite>Chess</cite>. I remember asking my mom to buy that for me. I still have that single.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Any other early musical memories? Maybe something that inspired you to make music?

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> I don’t know how it went down for me; my parents weren’t very into music. I can remember going to see lots of bad family theater stuff in Branson; uh, a lot of that. And&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;yeah; I don’t know; I was always kind of just fascinated by […] the media […]; the way that records felt in my hand and digging through piles of what people thought was junk to find gold. That was part of the reason that I started a record store, that I had a ridiculous collection of music by the time I was twenty. I’m still that way.

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-08-14-shitty-awesome/czahller-20121231-22-25-13.jpg", "img-constrained", "Derek Lemke plays guitar at OPOLIS, New Year’s Eve", "Derek (II)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

I can’t think of any specific&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I saw some band that called themselves the Beach Boys at Frontier City in <time datetime="1990">1990</time>, but if it had any members of the original Beach Boys, I couldn’t tell. I think <span class="h-card p-name">Mike Love</span> might have been in the band. I remember some band that called themselves the <span class="h-card p-org">Monkees</span>, and didn’t have <span class="h-card p-name">Davy Jones</span>, and I was like, <q>Okay …</q>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Anyone else?

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> Why do you play guitar, Derek?

<span class="spkr">Lemke</span> So I can get chicks.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Does it work?

<span class="spkr">Lemke</span> Not really. Um; I don’t know; I just remember listening to my dad’s records when I was&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;<span class="h-card p-name">Tom Petty</span>; I remember [<cite>Damn the Torpedoes</cite>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/damn-torpedoes-deluxe-version/id395240013?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and, like, <span class="h-card p-org">Black Sabbath</span>, [<cite>We Sold Our Soul for Rock ’n’ Roll</cite>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/black-sabbath/id165907?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. I just really liked music, and I asked for a guitar [when] I was six.

It was a crappy acoustic Harmony. I took lessons for […] six months, and the teacher was trying to teach me blues, when I wanted to play rock ’n’ roll. [I] didn’t quite get that concept, that young. I wanted to play <cite class="short-work">Eruption</cite> when I was six. [*laughter*] Then I quit.

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> My guitar teacher, the first song he tried to teach me was <cite class="short-work">Ain’t Talkin’ ’bout Love,</cite> and I didn’t know what it was, because I […] really didn’t care about <span class="h-card p-org">Van Halen</span>, and he didn’t play it; it was a weird lesson; he’s like, <q>Here; here’s the tabs for this. I’m gonna teach you to play this song.</q> And he, like, never played the song for me, because it was like, pre-&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I guess my teacher after that guy had tapes, but, uh, that guy didn’t have shit in his room [to play a recording of the song].

I used to listen to&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;my parents *loved* music; they’re super into music. My mom […] painted her room purple and had a black light and listened to <span class="h-card p-name">Janice Joplin</span>. My dad played guitar, so music was always in our house. And they bought and bought records all the time; they had, like, I don’t know&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;a couple hundred records?&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;and some 8-tracks!

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You’ve cited musical influences and styles in other interviews, but here are some things I think I hear: 70s punk, 60s garage, 80s garage revival, the <span class="h-card p-org">Cramps</span>, the <span class="h-card p-org">Ramones</span>, <span class="h-card p-name">Phil Spector</span>. Care to add anything?

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> Yeah, I love “girl groups.” Yeah, when we first started, that “tinny” sound we wanted, and that *really* lo-fi [sound] was&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;the shitty first recordings from [Detroit punk band] [<span class="h-card p-org">Tyvek</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/tyvek/id252039028?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} were a major thing, were the thing that all three of us want to sound like. And they *were* shitty/awesome. We saw Tyvek at <span class="small-caps">OPOLIS</span> and that was the beginning; that was the true genesis of the band.

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-08-14-shitty-awesome/czahller-20121231-22-23-31.jpg", "img-constrained", "Joey Powell sings into the microphone, New Year’s Eve at OPOLIS. He wears a full beard & sunglasses, a dark suit with a crisp white shirt, and a black tie with a vertical crimson stripe.", "Joey (I)" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> I remember talking to [Tyvek frontman] <span class="h-card p-name">Kevin Boyer</span> at a show in Lawrence, Kansas, and confessing drunkenly that *we* had started a band that was […] a direct rip-off of Tyvek, and he was really happy about that, and then [he] blushed and said, <q>That’s funny, because our band was supposed to be a direct ripoff of the [<span class="h-card p-org">Motards</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/yo-love-mexico/id595928928?i=595929030&uo=4&at=10ldh){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.</q>&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;er, not the Motards; the <span class="h-card p-org">Mummies</span>!

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You’ve released more than one version of several songs: <cite class="short-work" href="https://shittyawesome.bandcamp.com/">Say So,</cite> <cite class="short-work" href="https://shittyawesome.bandcamp.com/">Hang Up,</cite> <cite class="short-work" href="https://shittyawesome.bandcamp.com/">Shreds,</cite> <cite class="short-work" href="https://shittyawesome.bandcamp.com/">Birthday Suit,</cite> and <cite class="short-work" href="https://shittyawesome.bandcamp.com/">Bear with Chainsaw Hands (Billy Crystal Meth).</cite> Why did you remake those songs?

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> The [songs] from the [self-titled] cassette release&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;it was […] so that we could have something to sell at a table. I mean, we only manufactured 50 copies of them, so we knew they were never going to make it into anyone’s hands.

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> Also, we wanted wanted you to be able to listen [to each of the releases] and not be like, <q>Oh, *this* was recorded *here*, and *this* was recorded in another place.</q> It was for continuity. But it’s cool; because you can […] hear the evolution of our sound.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Your cassette single, a 7&Prime; EP, and an album were recorded at three different locations, with three different producers and recording engineers, but they all have a consistently lo-fi sound. The vocals are buried pretty deep in the mix; I had trouble understanding the lyrics when I listened on my computer speakers. What’s your thinking on the lo-fi æsthetic?

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> We never wanted it to sound *too* good.

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> We’ve also got a reputation as the most *efficient* band, for both our shows: our load-on, load-off; and for recording. Everybody we’ve worked with, it’s like, <q>Man, ya’ll are in-and-out!</q>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I read that you recorded your full-length in just four hours.

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> We recorded our full-length in four hours.

<span class="spkr">Lemke</span> It was like, seventeen songs in four hours?

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> It was us, and <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Chris</span> [<span class="p-family-name">Harris</span></span> at Hook Echo Sound], and the leftovers from a keg from my birthday party.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I love the <span class="h-card p-org">Go-Gos</span> parody cover on the [<cite>Hang Up</cite>](https://shittyawesome.bandcamp.com/album/hang-up-7){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} EP; it’s absolutely brilliant. How did that come about?

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> Well, we took our clothes off and put some towels on. [*laughter*] That was Travis’s idea.

<span class="spkr">Lemke</span> It was Travis.

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> [The photographer] was [<span class="h-card p-name">Christian Pitt</span>](https://www.saatchionline.com/xianpitt){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. Yeah; she did it all.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Except for Travis, I think you all have previous or concurrent musical projects. Could you tell me about that, including Joey, since he’s not here?

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> Joey’s in two bands right now, neither of which have played a show yet.

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> But they’ve got a show&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;[<span class="h-card p-org">Tumbling Nebulæ</span>](/blog/tumbling-nebulae-interview-gallery/) has a show scheduled September 14<sup>th</sup> at the <span class="small-caps">OPOLIS</span> with a couple of other bands.

He’s also in a band with the [<span class="h-card p-org">Copperheads</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/copperheads/id569307483?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;<span class="h-card p-name">Andy Escobar</span> and <span class="h-card p-name">Dane Kitchens</span>. They’re calling themselves the <span class="h-card p-org">Shutdown Shutouts</span>.

<span class="spkr">Lemke</span> I play guitar for <span class="h-card p-org">Depth &amp; Current</span>. That’s the main one. I’ve been talking to a few other people about doing some stuff, but nothing’s […] set in stone.

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> Has the <span class="h-card p-org">Gross Beast</span> been slayed?

<span class="spkr">Lemke</span> I guess I did play in Gross Beast for two shows.

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> I’m actually working on a project, sort of a twee [<span class="h-card p-org">Belle and Sebastian</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/belle-and-sebastian/id2989382?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, [<span class="h-card p-org">Camera Obscura</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/camera-obscura/id17885376?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} feel, with people [who] I don’t want to mention yet. And a [Cramps](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/the-cramps/id93124?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} tribute band; that’s coming up.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> What’s currently playing on your iPods or turntables?

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> I’ve been excited that in the last week, I’ve brought home two [<span class="h-card p-name">Janet Jackson</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/janet-jackson/id1272779?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} LPs, and I’ve been listening to the new [<span class="h-card p-org">Washed Out</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/paracosm-bonus-track-version/id655768700?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} record a lot.

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> [*laughing*] You’re listening to <span class="h-card p-name">Janet Jackson</span>?

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> Yeah; I bought her first two albums; they sound a lot like [<span class="h-card p-name">Michael Jackson</span>’s] [<cite>Off the Wall</cite>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/off-the-wall/id186166282?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

The last record I listened to on my turntable was yesterday, and it was a Folkways collection of French Provençal&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;kind of precursor to <span class="h-card p-name">Joan Baez</span>, but in the French countryside. I also listened to a Black Sabbath record. […]

<span class="spkr">Lemke</span> I listened to <span class="h-card p-org">Weekend</span>’s[^1] [new album](https://music.apple.com/us/album/jinx/1321260811?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}; it’s really good. Uh, <span class="h-card p-org">Cold Cave</span>’s [<cite>Cherish the Light Years</cite>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/cherish-light-years-bonus/id425679711?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. Some <span class="h-card p-name">Danny Brown</span>: [<cite>XXX</cite>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/xxx-deluxe-version/id508262167?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

[^1]: The New York&ndash;via&ndash;San Francisco shoegaze trio, not the Canadian singer-songwriter.

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> Uh, *I* am really hot for this band that I just discovered, even though they released their record in 2003, called [<span class="h-card p-org">Ashrae Fax</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/ashrae-fax/id610496343?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. <span class="h-card p-org">Mexican Summer</span> just reissued their debut album&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;their only album, I guess. I’ve been really hot on that. And I was listening to this Italian guy, [<span class="h-card p-name">Pino d’Angiò</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/pino-dangio/id258216869?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}; he’s like, this Italian crooner. And some [<span class="h-card p-name">Diana Dors</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/diana-dors/id253061304?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and [<span class="h-card p-name">Buffy Sainte-Marie</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/buffy-sainte-marie/id182972?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. […] The new [<span class="h-card p-org">Zola Jesus</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/versions/id667027545?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} is on [my playlist] too. I’ve listened to that a *lot*.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I’d like you each to redeem a song: name a song that doesn’t get much respect, but that you really love, and tell me what makes it so great.

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> I keep having conversations about the first [<span class="h-card p-org">Def Leppard</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/def-leppard/id117554?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} record. Def Leppard; I know. Total bullshit later on; but, man! <cite>On through the Night</cite>; whoo! That record is new wave, British heavy-metal perfectness.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Is there one track that stands out for you?

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> No; I’m not very good at paying attention to the names of songs at this point. I just listen to albums start-to-finish. […]

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> [<span class="h-card p-name">Klaus Nomi</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/klaus-nomi/id303724325?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} is a go-to for me, [who] drives people crazy. I love his rendition of <cite class="short-work">You Don’t Own Me</cite>; I think it’s amazing. I love that song, first of all; but his singing is&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I don’t know; it’s, like, kind of *angelic* to me. And … it puts me in a happy place; it’s a visceral thing. And it’s sad; he’s really sad. He&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;he is&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;he’s a male girl group […].

<span class="spkr">Lemke</span> I listen to a lot of shitty, over-produced pop, like [<span class="h-card p-name">Taylor Swift</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/taylor-swift/id159260351?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. <cite class="short-work">[We Are Never Ever Getting Back Together](https://itunes.apple.com/us/album/we-are-never-ever-getting/id571445253?i=571445457&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite> is pretty solid. I mean, it’s just a great pop song. It’s got hooks; production’s amazing on it. A lot of people don’t expect that from me.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Why not?

<span class="spkr">Lemke</span> ’cause I play in Shi++ty/Awesome.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Well, that’s the *obvious* reason.

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> Because he’s a vampire. [*laughter*]

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> What’s in the future for Shi++ty/Awesome? Can we expect a reunion show?

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> Uh, it could happen. We’re calling this the last show and I think we’re&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> We are not averse to playing together again. And for us, it’s really easy. We hadn’t practiced or played together […] for at least four months. And we got back together […] and we had practice a couple of weeks ago, and&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;it’s funny, because when we were playing more, sometimes, we would have to have a few warmup songs […]. But, we got together and we just played the set. And we nailed it.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You sounded tight last night when I came over to the record store [to photograph the rehearsal].

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> We’ll try to fuck it up a little, my friend. [*laughter*]

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> Yeah; put seven drinks in each of them and we’ll see. [*laughter continues*]

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> That’s pretty good, too; because we were all hungover&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I dunno; *I* was pretty hungover.

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> I thinks Shi++ty/Awesome, at this point&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;it’s been kind of interesting the last couple of weeks, especially last night, to go, <q>Aw, shit; this may be the last practice we ever have.</q> Um, yeah. Derek and Joey […] are currently in other bands; for me, personally, it’s the first band I’ve ever been in. I *almost* learned how to play an instrument, just to be in this band. And so I’m at the point where I’m starting to think, <q>Oh, man; this might be the last live show I ever play,</q>, and it’s a little […] scary […]. I think Will might be having a tiny bit of that, because he’s not already in another band, and the process of starting another band […] is a little daunting.

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> I’m just gonna take my karaoke tracks and do solo karaoke.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Last thoughts?

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> We’ve played with a lot of really good bands.

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> We’ve gotten a lot of&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I don’t want to say, *special* treatment, but, because we were friends with the <span class="small-caps">OPOLIS</span>, we got to do a lot of stuff that other bands might not&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;.

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> Well, not just the <span class="small-caps">OPOLIS</span>; we’re friends with a lot of people who book a lot of shows. And … we’ve been very fortunate to play with some of our favorite bands.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Is there one that stands out, that you’ll take to your grave the fact that you got to play with them?

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> I would say [<span class="h-card p-org">Nobody</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/nobody/id3824806?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> I have two; the [<span class="h-card p-org">Lightning Bolt</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/lightning-bolt/id3208711?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} show, and the <span class="small-caps">OPOLIS</span> show where we got to play with [The] [<span class="h-card p-org">Olivia Tremor Control</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/the-olivia-tremor-control/id2872017?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> [That] was amazing. [Also]; I really liked playing with [<span class="h-card p-org">Bleached</span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/bleached/id479947369?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> The house show that we played at SXSW and there were only ten people in the audience, and it was <span class="h-card p-name">[<span class="p-nickname">Black Joe Lewis</span></span>](https://itunes.apple.com/us/artist/black-joe-lewis/id264480554?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and his band.

<span class="spkr">Muir</span> I looked up, and Black Joe Lewis was there! ’cause I kind of zone-out when I’m playing. [*laughter*] And then I looked and fuckin’ Black Joe Lewis is groovin’ out to&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;and like, for the whole thing; he only left when we had, like, two songs left; he had to go get ready [for his set]. But he totally fuckin’ rocked-out to our band! That was cool as shit.

<span class="spkr">Searle</span> We definitely got to play some fun shows.
