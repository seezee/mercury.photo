Tumbling Nebulæ formed in early <time datetime="2013">2013</time> when bassist <span class="h-card p-name">Steven Machovic</span> and keyboard player <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Cynthia</span> <span class="p-nickname">“Luxy”</span> <span class="p-family-name">Machovic</span></span> got together with a couple of other musicians who should be familiar to anyone following Norman’s indie music scene. Guitarist <span class="h-card p-name">Joey Powell</span> was the guitar player for the late, lamented [<span class="h-card p-org">Shi++y Awesome</span>](/blog/shittyawesome-post-mortem/), while <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Jason</span> <span class="p-nickname">“Lightsmith”</span> <span class="p-family-name">Scott</span></span> has played drums for the <span class="h-card p-org">Mean Spirits</span> and occasionally substitutes with <span class="h-card p-org">Em and the Mother Superior</span>.

Steven, Cynthia, and Jason dropped by the Bureau in October (shortly after playing their first show) for drinks and an interview (as was the case when we interviewed Shi++y Awesome, Joey had to work and could not make it). We talked about boozy cupcakes, a mania for Japanese cars, and heavy basses and fast drums.

<span class="spkr">Mercury Photo Bureau</span> What are your musical backgrounds?

<span class="spkr">Steven Machovic</span> It started in the late 80s, early 90s, when the [Red Hot Chili Peppers](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/red-hot-chili-peppers/id889780?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and [Primus](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/primus/id105588?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} got pretty big&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;ever since then I wanted to play bass. I just like the sound&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I mean, you can do slap and pop and picking.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I take it you’re only interested in the electric bass&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;you wouldn’t play the double-bass?

<span class="spkr">Steven</span> Um, possibly.

<span class="spkr">Jason Scott</span> You do use a bow.

<span class="spkr">Steven</span> True. I asked my parents, and they got me an old Peavey T-40 bass, which I got rid of. Which I’m kicking myself in the ass for that now, because they’re pretty popular now&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;they’re vintage and made in the U.S.A. They’re actually good basses&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;they’re just twenty pounds; that’s all.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Luxy?

<span class="spkr">Cynthia “Luxy” Machovic</span> I’ve been playing piano since I was about twelve years old. Starting off I did a lot of classical. I think it was about ’95 or ’96 when I got into electric keys. Coming into the late ’90s or early 2000s I got into synthesizers. Big influences were [Squarepusher](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/squarepusher/id4514377?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, [Boards of Canada](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/boards-of-canada/id2989314?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, uh, lots of different sounds. It’s like, a lot of&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;uh, bass-ey, drum, synth; not like club music: it’s more of an ambient sound. Another influence is old [M83](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/m83/id46086389?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;the [<cite href="https://music.apple.com/us/album/dead-cities-red-seas-lost-ghosts/896424792?app=music&ign-itsct=896424792-896424792&ign-itscg=0177&ign-mpt=uo%3D4&at=10ldhk">Dead Cities, Red Seas & Lost Ghosts</cite>](https://music.apple.com/us/album/dead-cities-red-seas-lost-ghosts/896424792?app=music&ign-itsct=896424792-896424792&ign-itscg=0177&ign-mpt=uo%3D4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} album. [<span class="h-card p-name">Bj&ouml;rk</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/bjork/id295015?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} also got me into those sounds.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Jason?

<span class="spkr">Jason</span> I’ve always been around [music]. My dad was a drummer, so I grew up watching him play in country &amp; western bands and southern-rock bands. I got my first little, tiny kit when I was five, and I’d sit next to him and bang on it. I really picked it up in earnest when I was twelve&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;my mom tried to get me to play saxophone for a few years, but then I became friends with drummers and I just needed to get drums. So, my dad located an old ’70s clear, orange [kit]&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;you know, like [<span class="h-card p-name">John Bonham</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/john-bonham/id649167?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} would play in the ’70s&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I really got into that heavy [Led Zeppelin](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/led-zeppelin/id994656?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} sound&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;—

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I don’t remember from the show&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;do you play two bass drums like Bonham?[^1]

[^1]: We’ve since learned that Bonham used a Ludwig Speed King Pedal and a single bass drum.

<span class="spkr">Jason</span> No; but I used to play a lot of fast bass drum stuff that&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I mean, I had some friends in the audience say, <q>Oh, get off the double bass drum pedals,</q> and then they’d realize, <q>Oh; […] he’s just playing really fast.</q> Growing up I listened to what my dad was into&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;the [Beach Boys](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/the-beach-boys/id562555?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and [Queen](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/queen/id3296287?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and [ELO](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/electric-light-orchestra/id471528?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, and then Led Zeppelin; then it turned into the [Beatles](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/the-beatles/id136975?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

I got my first CD player in ’92 and started buying modern stuff: from twelve until I was twenty I was into [Rage against the Machine](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/rage-against-the-machine/id899409?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and [Nirvana](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/nirvana/id112018?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and all that grunge stuff.

But, here’s the thing: although I was doing percussion in high school, I was teaching myself the drumset by listening to the records. So, there were a lot of 3/11 [time signatures]&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I’m getting to a point [*laughter*]&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;and there’s some pretty poor taste in there, but looking back I don’t regret it, because there was good musicianship in there; there’s good teaching about different styles.

Then I played in a&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I don’t know, new metal type band?&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;around <time datetime="2000">2000</time>. They were called <strong>Communication Lost</strong>; they were kind of a staple at [Mr.] Bill’s and some of the crappier bars in the metro. Some time during that I was breaking [my] knuckles, breaking drumsticks, breaking equipment through poor technique. So I took some lessons, and all that kind of busy-ness moved from the bottom on the bass drums up to my hands, which made me sound more back-beat, more ’60s, so then I got into that kind of music.

So the drumming led me to the [Kinks](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/the-kinks/id1179227?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and, like, this kinda copycat band out of Florida, [We the People](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/we-the-people/id191274225?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, that I really like. It’s surf rock, ’though from the other coast; you know, all about girls and cars and stuff. But it’s really neat.

Around <time datetime="2003">2003</time> I got into a lot of busy math rock, like the [Mars Volta](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/the-mars-volta/id1645800?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, but then I started playing with the <strong>Mean Spirits</strong> around <time datetime="2005">2005</time>, and people started comparing us to the <strong>Kinks</strong> and to that musical era.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Steven and Luxy, how did you meet?

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> Steven and I met online in <time datetime="2005">2005</time>, on a Subaru car forum.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I used to drive a Subaru station wagon!

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> I never had one; I just dated guys that drove Subarus. A guy I used to date back in Austin had a Subaru, and introduced me to the whole Subaru world. And I really, really liked the vehicles, but I didn’t have one, so I dated guys that had them so I could drive them. I moved up to New Jersey, where Steven and I had been talking already for years, just as friends on the [Subaru] board, and that’s the only way I knew him. Never knew what he looked like; just knew his handle.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> What was it?

<span class="spkr">Steven</span> 02-GGA. That was the model year and production code of my Subaru wagon.

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> So, on one of the threads, we were just posting pictures of everybody, and I saw him. And me being the weak-in-the-knees-for-Japanese-boys-type, I said, <q>Oh, my god, <q>hi to you!</q>&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;who is this guy?</q> And I found out it’s the guy I’ve known for years and years. And we decided to hang out, and so it went from there. It was long distance for about a year while he was in Virginia Beach and I was in New Jersey, before I moved down. Eventually I moved in and we lived together for a few years.

<span class="spkr">Steven</span> I was in the navy; I got out in <time datetime="2008">2008</time>. [After a brief stay in New Mexico,] I got a job in Norman and we moved here.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> How’d the band form?

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> Me and my drunken escapades. I think it started with my cupcakes, with Jason. I own Luxy Cupcakes; they’re cupcakes for adults&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;we make spicey, boozy, sweet and savory cupcakes. We deal with Native Roots Market in [Oklahoma City’s] Deep Deuce, and what’s funny is, Jason’s good friends with [Native Roots owners] <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Matt</span></span> and <span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-given-name">Sarah</span></span>.

<span class="spkr">Jason</span> So, Matt and I would hang out and drink and then raid his store for food, and he’d have her cupcakes, and I would buy a few of those and chow down. And he would keep me up-to-date, ’cause this was every other Saturday, so he would let me know, <q>Here’s when they’re coming in; here’s what they’re gonna be,</q> and [he’d] hold some back for me. I was a fan. Then I met her in the store and had my little fanboy moment, [*affects high-pitched little girl voice*] <q>Oh, my god; you’re the ’Luxy’!</q>

Then I saw the opportunity to barter [product] photography for cupcakes. So I gave her the card, and I don’t think we saw each other until January [2013]; I had a photography show at Massive Graphics during the [2<sup>nd</sup> Friday Artwalk](https://2ndfridaynorman.com/){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, and Luxy popped in with Steven.

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> What’s funny is, just before we met up with [Jason], we ran into Joey at Guestroom [Records], and we were talking about random stuff, and we talked about [wanting to start a band and needing] a drummer, and he goes, <q>Go see Jason; he’s really good! You guys have got to go talk to him.</q> [*speaking to Jason*] This was when we first heard about you. So we were pumped up; we literally *ran* to Massive, because Joey said you weren’t going to be there for very long.

So we go in there, and we see him, and I’m like, <q>I think that’s Jason; I think that’s Jason,</q> so we talk, and he gives me his card, and I looked at it, and it’s like, <q>This looks *so* familiar; why do I remember this card?</q> It was from the cupcakes!

Then one night Joey and I were drinking at Abner’s [Alehouse], and we had this moment where I told him, <q>Joey you’re one of the greatest guitarists we’ve met in Norman; we love your style; you’re fun; be our guitarist!</q> So Joey was like, <q>Yeah, yeah; I’ll be your guitarist,</q> drunk as can be, just like I was, but in the back of my mind, I’m like, <q>I’ve gotta remember this; the one thing I need to remember is to find Joey after tonight.</q> A couple of days later, I found him and I said, <q>Joey. You *said* you’re gonna be our guitarist,</q> and he’s like, <q>What?</q> That was this spring.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Jason, how do you know Joey and his magnificent beard?

<span class="spkr">Jason</span> And his magnificent beard&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;which he’s had a *long* time. He and I go a long way back. We both went to the same high school; we both were in the same band program.

We were both loosely affiliated with McFarlin Methodist Church in Norman; they would have this week at the church, where all the kids would live there&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;during the school year; all the classes were there. It had a <cite>Lord of the Flies</cite> feel, because it seemed there were never any adults around, even though they were. And no one was killing anybody. [*laughter*]

We started hanging out then. It’s funny; that youth group, and especially our age group: a lot of musicians came out of that. <span class="h-card p-name">Scott Twitchell</span> from [Depth &amp; Current](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/depth-current/id517299616?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}; couple of the guys in [Dikes of Holland](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/dikes-of-holland/id347453614?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} in Austin were in the McFarlin youth group. It’s funny; we’ve played in bars together and I look at these guys and I think, <q>We all met in church!</q>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Luxy, how’d you come to be a cupcake baker?

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> At the job I used to work at in New Mexico, there was a cupcake place around the corner. And they were about 3 dollars a cupcake; and I would buy two a day, because I have a real sweet tooth. Well, after a few months, Steven looked at the bank account and did *not* like what he saw. So he told me that I couldn’t buy any more cupcakes. So I learned how to bake my own.

I’m not a fan of just plain ol’ vanilla and chocolate; I’m an adult. I want *beer* in my cupcake, and Steven wanted spicy, and, why not throw some bacon in there? So we started experimenting, and it took us about three to four years to get the recipes the way we want them. It was a lot of trial and error, because baking is chemistry. We went from too dry to just as moist as can be.

Steven came up with a lot of our spicy and bacon recipes, like the Ghost Pepper Chili and the Pineapple Habañero. The one that I’m proud of is the <span lang="it" class="foreign">cibo matto</span>, which is named after one of our favorite bands. It means <q>crazy food</q> in Italian. It’s a lemon cake with a sweet <span lang="it">marscapone</span> and basil filling with a candied <span lang="it">pancetta</span> on top. That’s Steve’s favorite.

<span class="spkr">Jason</span> That’s *my* favorite, too.

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> My favorite is the Almond Joy Division; and you can tell, some of our cupcakes are named after bands we like. Anybody orders that, I always make extra for myself.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Jason, you’re a photographer?

<span class="spkr">Jason</span> I normally shoot things that hold still. Landscapes, architecture; I like structure. I also like seeing landscape reclaim architecture. And bands, which don’t hold still. I like to use a rear shutter flash, which gets some of the movement leading into it, so the image makes you feel like you were there; you have the lights and the action and the craziness.

I shot a lot when I was surveying;[^2] it afforded me the opportunity to be all over Oklahoma in some pretty rural locations, where if you see something and you have a camera on you, it’s nice and handy. I guess I got the bug&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I did a lot of shooting in Germany and when I was in high school.

I got my first digital camera in <time datetime="2003">2003</time> and moved to Santa Barbara a week later. You can’t look anywhere in Santa Barbara without seeing a good picture to take. I had some friends, but not a big social life, so I had a lot of time to cruise around on a bike and take pictures.

[^2]: Jason used to work for a surveying company.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> How did you arrive at the band name?

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> [*laughter*] It’s changed! The first name was <strong>Coriolis Effect</strong>. Which was a U.K. band that someone didn’t check on the internet! [*laughter*] The other one was <strong>Low Cloud 9</strong>.

<span class="spkr">Steven</span> Since I’m a meteorologist, “low cloud 9” is the classification for a thunderstorm.

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> <strong>Tumbling Nebul&aelig;</strong> is a reference from <cite>[Doctor Who](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_Terrors_(Doctor_Who)){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite>. He does say <q>tumbling nebulas,</q> not <q>tumbling nebulæ.</q> I majored in astronomy&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;cosmology&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;so I like everything “space.”

<blockquote cite="https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b014983t">

Through crimson stars and silent stars and tumbling nebulas like oceans set on fire. Through empires of glass and civilizations of pure thought. And a whole terrible wonderful universe of impossibilities.

  <footer>&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;<cite class="cite-person">The Doctor</cite></footer>
</blockquote>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> In your band bio, you describe the band as <q>post-rock,</q> but that’s just about as vague as can be. What do you mean?

<span class="spkr">Jason</span> It’s spacey; it’s instrumental, for the most part; a little bit heavy, but, you know, a lot of dynamics. It’s not real “pop”-y.

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> <cite>Gamma Ray Burst</cite> is a little “pop”-ier.

What I want&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;you know, I’m actually learning the bass and guitar right now, so, instead of getting an extra player, I’m playing those *and* the synthesizer. Jason likes “busy.” I like that too, but we’ll have some songs where there’s a slow part and Jason’s just “chill”&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;kinda takin’ a breather.

<span class="spkr">Jason</span> You want to feel like something big, walkin’ through the woods, just lumbering, and not be too loud or too busy; [you want to] give the song some *weight*.

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> And then come in with good sounds, you know. When I play the synth, I’m gonna be using a fuzz pedal; I’m gonna be using a delay, a reverb, tremolo; I’m gonna be playing with all these different sounds, where it really doesn’t sound like a synthesizer.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> When I saw your first show, you played four songs for a total of about forty minutes, non-traditional forms, in fact, structurally free-form&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;inchoate, if that’s a fair description&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;—

<span class="spkr">Jason</span> Especially that time; I think we rearranged a lot of stuff on stage that night&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;<q>Oh; so this is where we’re going?</q>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> So, related to the previous question, it’s instrumental; it doesn’t have an easily discernable structure unless you’ve heard it before (which, obviously, for that audience, they have *never* heard it before). What’s the motivation to make this kind of music?

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> I’ve always liked instrumental music, based on my own musical background&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;classical and ambient. I’ve always played alone; I did all my own sounds; again, instrumental. I “got into” a lot of bands, and a lot of *them* were instrumental.

<span class="spkr">Jason</span> [In school,] I was in band, full orchestra, jazz band. I really, really, *really* always loved <cite>Fantasia</cite>. And I’d never played in an instrumental band, so [Steve and Luxy] said that [they were forming an instrumental band], and I said, <q>Oh, fantastic! I’ve always wanted to do that!</q>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> What are your future plans? [*Discussion of various schedules ensues*&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;Ed.]

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> We’re all working.

<span class="spkr">Jason</span> We’re all adults without kids, who have jobs with crazy hours.

<span class="spkr">Steven</span> I think a regional tour would be nice.

<span class="spkr">Jason</span> I haven’t discussed it with these guys, but the last couple of bands I’ve played in, we employed an eight to eight-and-a-half hour circle around Oklahoma City [that we were willing to travel]. You can hit St. Louis, lots of good “party” schools in Arkansas, Austin, Houston, San Antonio, Denton. If you wanna stretch your rules, you can reach Kansas City; Columbia, Missouri; Lawrence. We probably need to add a song or two to get a good, solid, hour-long set.

<span class="spkr">Steven</span> I *do* want to play the [Norman Music Festival](https://www.normanmusicfestival.com/){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> What’s on your iPods or other music players?

<span class="spkr">Jason</span> I’ve been listening to a lot of [<span class="h-card p-name"><span class="p-nickname">Bombino</span></span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/bombino/id424622037?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. And last week, I went through an old-school [Metallica](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/metallica/id3996865?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} phase. Lot of garage rock: [Tame Impala](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/tame-impala/id290242959?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and [<span class="h-card p-name">Ty Segall</span>](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/ty-segall/id435800519?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> I’ve been on a Mexican rock kick, plus one ska band out of Argentina. [Café Tacvba](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/cafe-tacvba/id267781297?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, [Caifanes](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/caifanes/id168944293?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, [Jaguares](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/jaguares/id2426419?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} and [Jumbo](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/jumbo/id6710463?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} from Mexico. The Argentine band is called [Los Fabulosos Cadillacs](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/los-fabulosos-cadillacs/id300251?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

<span class="spkr">Steven</span> I’ve been listening to a lot of Japanese post-rock; there’s a band called [Miaou](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/miaou/id264058388?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, and [another band called] [MONO](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/mono/id172989390?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}. Little bit of [Icelandic band] [Múm](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/mum/id4504094?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Time for our regular feature, <cite>Reedeem a Song</cite>&trade;. Name a song you love that doesn’t get much respect, either critically or in the culture, and tell me what’s great about it.

<span class="spkr">Jason</span> <cite>[The Novelty of Haunting](https://music.apple.com/us/album/the-novelty-of-haunting/1470937395?i=375714980&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite></a> by <strong>So Many Dynamos</strong>. The vocals are pretty juvenile sounding. Not the lyrics; the way the singer’s voice sounds, like he’s fresh out of high school. He’s not; it’s just the way he sounds. The musicianship is great; the composition is great; throughout the whole album you get these thematic callbacks to earlier songs.

I really like the whole album. The song is about a guy who’s died and he’s a ghost, and he’s been haunting for a while, and the novelty’s worn off. And he kind of feels like a creeper, hanging out in his old friends’ houses, and he just wants to be with them.

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> I like the Icelandic band Múm, a song called <cite>[Weeping Rock](https://music.apple.com/us/album/weeping-rock/1495801573?i=1495801867&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite></a>. I’ve never heard a singer like that; it’s really high pitched. The sound, where it comes in *rough*; you know, with the different synths, and you can tell they’ve used these *random* pedals […] and then you have this […] angelic voice. It makes me feel really good.

<span class="spkr">Steven</span> There’s this group from New Hampshire, the [Super Secret Project](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/the-super-secret-project/id288396484?uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}, and they do a parody of the Jay-Z song <cite>Empire State of Mind</cite>, but it’s <cite>Granite State of Mind</cite>. I’m from New Hampshire, and it seems like anywhere else in the United States, New Hampshire doesn’t get much respect.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I’d say it’s more like it doesn’t even show up in most people’s consciousnesses. So why specifically do you like the song?

<span class="spkr">Steven</span> It has interesting facts […] about New Hampshire.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> So, it’s educational?

<span class="spkr">Steven</span> Yeah.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> [*sarcastically*] That is *so* much more appealing! [*laughter*]

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> It’s actually pretty catchy. I remember when [Steven] showed [the YouTube video] to me for the first time. It’s quite comical and it had a good beat.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Condidering the source material, I guess I’m not surprised. [*laughter*] Anything else you’d like to say before we wrap up?

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> I’m really happy that we got together; Steven and I have been planning on doing this for years, since before we were married. I kept on pushing him to play music, you know: <q>Do it; do it; don’t stop, for whatever reason,</q> and I’m glad that Steven had the “oomph” to go with it. And I’m glad my drunken escapades led me to meet the other guys, along with my cupcakes.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> They sort of go together; some of them are a little boozy.

<span class="spkr">Steven</span> I’m glad that we’re here in Norman.

<span class="spkr">Luxy</span> Norman has been very kind to us, musically, mentally; it’s great. It’s so laid back and the music scene is great.
