## Hope from the Ashes

<span class="h-card p-name">Sam Baker</span> has told the story of barely surviving a train bombing many times. The stark outline: <time datetime="1986-06-25">some three decades ago</time>, the thirty-two-year-old white-water rafting guide sits in a train car, heading to Machu Picchu. A bomb believed to be the handiwork of the <span class="foreign" lang="es">Sendero Luminoso</span> (“Shining Path”) guerrillas sits in a red backpack directly overhead. Poorly made, it sends most of its explosive force through the roof when it goes off. It still manages to kill the tourist family Baker is sitting with as well as four others, and injures dozens of others. Baker’s femoral artery is cut, but he doesn’t bleed to death thanks to dumb luck and the heroic efforts of the first-responders. But his journey into darkness has just begun, as he slips in and out of consciousness, enduring subdural and cranial bleeding, gangrene, and renal failure. He also loses all hearing in one ear and suffers severe hearing loss in the other. Then there’s the tinnitus&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;the ringing in his ears never stops.

<time datetime="1984">Eighteen years later</time> Baker releases the first in a trilogy of critically acclaimed albums, <cite>Mercy</cite>. He’s learned to play guitar left-handed, having lost several fingertips on his right in the blast. Needless to say, he’s been thinking about a lot of things in the intervening years, and he’s ready to tell the world, or anyone who will listen, about it.

<blockquote>
  <p>Baker’s music is simultaneously beautiful and broken, like cracked crockery.</p>

There’s a lot more to the story, and if you don’t know it, stop reading right now and go listen to [this <time datetime="2014-05-06T10:25:00-5:00">2014</time> interview](https://www.npr.org/2014/05/06/310089151/sam-baker-finding-grace-in-the-wake-of-destruction){target=_blank rel="external noopener"} with <span class="h-card"><span class="p-org">Fresh Air</span>’s <span class="p-name"></span>Terri Gross</span>. Seriously, stop reading and go listen.

Okay, are you back? Good. This writer knew none of what you now know when he was assigned to photograph Baker at the Crystal Theatre in <time datetime="2013-07-13T17:00:00-5:00">2013</time>. All I knew was that I was in the presence of something special. I’m not the only one. He’s been called <q>[&hellip;] the most captivating songwriter in America.</q>[^1]

[^1]: <cite class="h-card p-name cite-person">Skanse, Richard</cite>, <cite>Lone Star Music</cite>, <cite class="short-work">[Sam Baker: <cite>Say Grace</cite>](https://lonestarmusicmagazine.com/sam-baker-say-grace/){target=_blank rel="external noopener"}</cite> (<time datetime="2013-07-01">1 July 2013</time>)

It was Baker’s ninth time to play Woodyfest; with the exception of <time datetime="2014">2014</time>, he’s played every festival since <time datetime="2005">2005</time>. Baker was accompanied by violinist <span class="h-card p-name">Megan Palmer</span>&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;herself no stranger to Woodyfest&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;and bassist <span class="h-card p-name">Erik Alvar</span>. <span class="h-card p-name">Don Conoscenti</span> later joined them.

<time datetime="2018-07-13T19:45:00-5:00">This year’s festival Friday</time>, Baker played the main stage at the Pastures of Plenty. Don Conoscenti once again provided guitar and vocals. Joining them on keyboard, accordion, and vocals was festival regular <span class="h-card p-name">Radoslav “Rad” Lorković</span>. Despite the early evening heat, the sizable crowd enthusiastically welcomed Baker&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;many in the audience knew what to expect, and were eager to witness it.
