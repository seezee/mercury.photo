# {{ title }}

Beloved Campus Corner eatery under new ownership</stack-l></hgroup>
<!-- markdownlint-enable MD025 --><mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "featured", "", "Angelo stands behind a seated Helen.", "Angelo and Helen", "eager" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

The aroma of grilling meat and potatoes frying in <span class="foreign" lang="el">gyros</span> grease permeates the block around <span class="h-card p-org">The Greek House</span>, guaranteeing a packed restaurant on most days. But soon, the <span class="h-card p-org">Campus Corner</span> institution will have a new owner. After placing the restaurant on the market <time datetime="2009">three years ago</time>, <span class="h-card p-name p-given-name">Angelo</span> and <span class="h-card p-name">Helen Dimas</span> have finally sold the beloved eatery.{.drop-cap}

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2012-09-27-greek-house/czahller-20120926-11-32-50.jpg", "", "Helen reviews an order while Angelo slices meat and an employeed fills orders behind her.", "Retiring owner Helen Dimas reviews an order", "large" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

After thirty-three years of slicing meat from a vertical spit and taking customer orders, Angelo and his wife Helen are retiring. They’ve always closed the restaurant during the Christmas and New Year holidays to visit family in Greece; now they will be moving back home. Their son, <span class="h-card p-name p-given-name">Bill</span>, who will remain at the restaurant for the time being, graduated from the University of Oklahoma (OU) with a masters degree in ærospace engineering in <time datetime="1989">1989</time> before coming to work in his parents’ restaurant.

The restaurant’s new owner, who goes by “<span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">Moe,</span>” was working behind the counter along with Angelo, Helen, and Bill when we dropped by yesterday for lunch. The family kindly allowed us to document one day of Angelo and Helen’s final week at the restaurant. The pair also took time out for informal portraits that evening, despite the early dinner rush.

Moe, a slight, friendly man with a reflexive smile, was concerned that his camouflage print T-shirt might not photograph well, so he opted to change. As you can see, Angelo did not; tending the <span class="foreign" lang="tr">döner kebabı</span> is hot work.

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2012-09-27-greek-house/czahller-20120926-17-31-37.jpg", "", "Moe & Angelo pose for the photographer in the restaurant’s back room. Moe has his arm over Angelo’s shoulder.", "New owner Moe and retiring owner Angelo.", "large" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

Angelo and Helen emigrated from <span lang="el">Prosymna</span>, Greece in <time datetime="1978">late 1978</time>, opening The Greek House <time datetime="1978-02">the following February</time>. The small Norman restaurant originally catered to the Iranian and Middle-Eastern students at OU. Eventually, other Norman residents discovered the place, which is known for large portions, low prices, and delicious flavors. Especially beloved by Normanites are the <span class="foreign" lang="el">gyros</span> (rotisserie broiled mixture of lamb and beef) and the accompanying traditional yoghurt sauce (“<span class="foreign" lang="el">Tzatziki</span>”).

The Greek House is located at 768 Jenkins Avenue near Boyd Street in Norman, Oklahoma. They open at <time datetime="10:30-6:00">10:30 a.m.</time> and close at <time datetime="20:30-6:00">8:30 p.m.</time> every day except Sunday. Bring cash or your checkbook; they don’t take credit cards.
