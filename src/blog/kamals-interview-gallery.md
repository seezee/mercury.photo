# {{ title }}

<!-- markdownlint-enable MD025 --><mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "featured", "", "The Kamals performing on the OPOLIS stage. Brad Nance (guitar, vox), Trey Allen (bass), Zachary Kaczka (guitar, vox), and Loren Williams (keys, vox). Not pictured: River Myers (drums).", "The Kamals", "eager" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

Five-piece fuzz rock outfit the <span class="h-card p-org">Kamals</span> (minus guitarist/backing vocalist <span class="h-card p-name">Brad Nance</span>) dropped by the Bureau in <time datetime="2013-01-20">January</time>, following their [rescheduled](/blog/concert-that-was-not/) appearance at Opolis in Norman. Described as a blend of hard rock, stoner rock, heavy psych, fuzz rock, and psychedelic rock styles,[^1] with the occasional foray into boogie rock and acoustic country-blues, the band’s sound is not of this era.

[^1]: <span class="h-card p-name">Goodman, Bill</span>. <cite class="short-work"><a href="https://thesodashop.wordpress.com/2012/04/19/introducing-the-kamals/" target="_blank" rel="external noopener noreferrer">Introducing … the Kamals</a>.</cite> <cite>The Soda Shop</cite>. <time datetime="2012-04-19T07:50-0500">19 April 2012.</time> Web. <time datetime="2013-02-11">11 February 2013</time>.

Indeed, it’s not of this century, as it draws heavily on the sounds of bands like the <span class="h-card p-org">Allman Brothers</span>, <span class="h-card p-org">Captain Beyond</span>, <span class="h-card p-org">Little Feat</span>, pre&ndash;<span class="h-card p-name">Michael McDonald</span> <span class="h-card p-org">Doobie Brothers</span>, <span class="h-card p-org">Lynyrd Skynyrd</span>, and ill-fated supergroup <span class="h-card p-org">Rhinoceros</span>. Their usual <span class="foreign" lang="la">modus operandi</span> is heavy, distorted guitar licks with lots of fuzz pedal, coupled with the naturally distorted sound of the Fender Rhodes electric piano.[^2] Top it all off with soulful harmony singing and the occasional wail of a mouth harp and you’ve got something pretty groovy.

[^2]: The Fender Rhodes blends acoustic mechanisms with electric pickups, not unlike an electric guitar.

Bespectacled and sporting a neatly trimmed goatee and short dreadlocks, guitarist <span class="h-card p-name">Zachary “Zak” Kaczka</span> sings lead in a rich, warm baritone. Bassist <span class="h-card p-name">Trey Allen</span> provides backing vocals, while keyboardist <span class="h-card p-name">Loren Williams</span> occasionally takes the lead vocal. Filling out the rhythm section is drummer <span class="h-card p-name">River Myers</span>, who remained mostly quiet during the interview in contrast to his chosen instrument. River was accompanied by his girlfriend <span class="h-card p-name p-given-name">Madi</span>. The group arrived at the cocktail hour, so after drinks[^3] we got down to business.

[^3]: CYA: No alcohol was served to minors.

<span class="spkr">Mercury Photo Bureau</span> Tell me about the band’s name.

<span class="spkr">Trey Allen</span> Yeah, it’s just kind of a thing we came up with. It happens to be an acronym of [our initials].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Is it pronounced <span class="ipa">/&#712;k&aelig;-m&#601lz/</span> or <span class="ipa">/k&#601;-&#712;m&#596;:lz/</span>?

<span class="spkr">Everyone</span> It’s <span class="ipa">/k&#601;-&#712;m&#596;:lz/</span>.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Please tell me your names, ages, and instruments&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;including you, Madi. [*laughter*]

<span class="spkr">River Myers</span> Alright, I’m River Myers; I play drums and I’m twenty.

<span class="spkr">Madi</span> I’m Madi&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;River’s girlfriend. I’m eighteen.

<span class="spkr">Loren Williams</span> I’m Loren Williams; I play keys, mainly a [Fender] Rhodes and an organ; I’m twenty-three.

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-02-11-kamals/czahller-20130112-23-19-02.jpg", "", "Zac sings & plays guitar at OPOLIS", "Zac (VI)", "large" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Zak Kaczka</span> I’m Zak; I play guitar, and I’m twenty-two.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> And you also sing. Loren sings too, right?

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> Yes.

<span class="spkr">Allen</span> I’m Trey; I’m twenty-two; I play bass.

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> You also sing.

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> Trey and Brad are the backups.

<span class="spkr">Madi</span>Brad Nance; he’s the other guitar player.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> How’d the band form?

<span class="spkr">Myers</span> Me and Loren were in a band, <span class="h-card p-org">Plaid Rabbit</span>.

<span class="spkr">Allen</span> Plus Brad, too; […] for three years […]. Before that, me, Zak and a couple of other friends of ours in high school had a band&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> Barn band.

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> We never did name it, but we ended up calling it barn band stuff because we recorded a little bit and we needed something to call it. Back in high school, we had a [hangout] that our friend’s mom let us [use. It was a sheet metal barn].

<span class="spkr">Allen</span> I think someone used to work on cars in it […].

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> We would hang out there after school and on weekends.

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> I played in the barn band, and then after [that], when Plaid Rabbit formed, I was in a band with my friends, <span class="h-card p-name p-given-name">Matt</span> and <span class="h-card p-name p-given-name">Bill</span>, and we played in <span class="h-card p-org">Vultures of Culture</span>. We only played a few shows; then that fizzled away, and then two or three years passed, and we got a house, called <q>Old Dog.</q>[^4] And then the Kamals formed, and it was fuckin’ awesome.

[^4]: [Old Dog Records](https://www.facebook.com/olddogrecords){target="_blank" rel="external noopener noreferrer"} is a recording studio in Oklahoma City.

<span class="spkr">Allen</span> I played in a few bands, here and there, and I played by myself a lot. I played guitar, and then I picked up bass for the Kamals. I actually like playing bass.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Okay, how did you actually meet these guys?

<span class="spkr">Allen</span> Ah, that was the barn.

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> We’ve all known each other, some of us, since middle school, some of us, since high school.

<span class="spkr">Madi</span> Seven years.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> When did you first pick up an instrument or become involved in music?

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-02-11-kamals/czahller-20130112-23-11-52.jpg", "", "River plays the drums at OPOLIS", "River (II)", "large" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Myers</span> I was twelve or thirteen, and right after I’d get out of school, they had all this musical equipment at the barn, so I’d go play Loren’s drums after school.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Are you self-taught?

<span class="spkr">Myers</span> Yeah; I tried to take lessons, but [they didn’t take].

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> My dad, [<span class="h-card p-name p-given-name">Edmund</span>,] played guitar since he was sixteen; [he] accumulated a lot of equipment, so it was always around. He was in a lot of cover bands […]. I just picked up a guitar one day […]; he saw that I was interested and […] he showed me a couple [of] things. And then when I wanted to get serious about it, he handed me a music theory book and a diagram of a C-major scale, and said, <q>That’s all you need to know.</q> […]

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> I started right around when I was thirteen, and I was gonna get a bass for Christmas. I’ve got an older brother, and I was like, <q>Man, I can’t wait to get a bass for Christmas,</q> and he was like, <q>I’m getting a bass for Christmas!</q> and I was like, <q>Man, I’m getting a guitar.</q> That’s how I wound up with a guitar.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Most people would have just gone for the guitar in the first place.

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> I don’t know what it is I like about the bass; I guess I just like the big clunkiness of it.

<span class="spkr">Allen</span> I used to skate and [be] really active. When I was thirteen, I cut my kneecap open, and, on my birthday, I asked for a guitar, because I was kind of laid up. I took one lesson, and that didn’t really work out, so I just [looked up guitar] tabs on the internet and kind of jammed around. I didn’t really get good […] until I got to the barn, when I was fifteen. And then I picked up bass […].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Does anyone have a favorite instrument?[^5]

[^5]: We meant the question to elicit an answer about a specific favorite instrument, e.g., <q>a hollow body Gretsch named ‘*Daisy*’ that I pried from a dead man’s fingers,</q> but Loren misconstrued.

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-02-11-kamals/czahller-20130112-22-59-12.jpg", "", "Loren, behind the keyboard, gazes stage right", "Loren (I)", "large" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> Drums. They’re really primal; you don’t need no music theory, although it helps as far as rhythmic patterns. You learn your eight beats, sixteen beats, stuff like that. It’s very easy to pick up and it’s a full body expression.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> How did you wind up being a keyboard player, then?

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> Well, I played guitar in Plaid Rabbit […], [and I had] played piano at my grandma’s house when I was a little kid, so I had a basic understanding, and then, at the barn, there was a little piano there that I used to mess around with […]. [When we formed the Kamals after Vultures of Culture broke up,] they were assigning instruments, and I got piano.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> You got the short straw.

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> Yeah.

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> Bullshit! That Rhodes is fuckin’ awesome!.

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> It’s awesome; it’s really sweet, but I wasn’t entirely comfortable with it. But I knew I could work something out with it, so it was like, <q>Oh, yeah, that’s fine.</q>

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Tell me about the Fender Rhodes. It’s not an instrument you hear much anymore; its heyday was in the late ’60s through the mid-’80s, mostly in the middle of the period. It’s got a really distinctive sound, but it’s big and heavy; it’s like carrying a Hammond B3 [organ] to shows. What gives it that sound, and how’d you wind up with it?

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> The Rhodes is Zak’s.

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> I’m a gearhead; I like buying shit. It’s bad.

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> Ever since [high school,] like, he had a custom cab, like a shitty amp sort of&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;I don’t know, the custom wasn’t that bad&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> It was terrible; that’s why I got a [Fender] Bassman.

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> So he always sold his stuff to get better things, and saved up his money […]. So anyway, [he got] the Rhodes, and we were all deciding what we were going to play […], and I was like, <q>What am I going to play?</q> and he was like, <q>I’ve got this Rhodes; you wanna play piano?</q> <q>Yeah, sure, I’ll play that.</q>

It’s kind of like electric guitar, a little bit, you know, you can distort the amp. And there’s guys lik, <span class="h-card p-name">Chick Corea</span> and <span class="h-card p-name">Herbie Hancock</span> who make the Rhodes have a certain style, like how [Jimi Hendrix does with a Fender or like Dick Dale does with a Stratocaster].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> On the opening track from your EP <cite>[Tell Me](https://music.apple.com/us/album/tell-me/id525269254?i=525269260&uo=4&at=10ldhk){target=_blank rel="external noopener}</cite> you’ve got what sounds like a Moog synth break. It reminds me of the intro to <span class="h-card p-name">Steve Miller</span>’s <cite class="short-work">Fly Like an Eagle.</cite> What is that?

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> That’s Brad’s flanger pedal.

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> *Mad scientist* Brad.

<span class="spkr">Allen</span> That’s a custom pedal he had made. He got it from one of his brother’s friends who made pedals; he was [having him test them out] and he eventually just let Brad keep ’em […].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Let’s talk about <cite class="short-work">Hands in the Water.</cite> It’s over fifteen minutes long; it’s made up of two acoustic vocal numbers bookending two acoustic instrumentals with long silences separating them.

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> That probably would have worked better if we’d had a vinyl release, because on the track listing, we could have just had <cite class="short-work">Hands in the Water,</cite> but then the record would have kept spinning […].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> So, you were thinking bonus tracks; it’s not meant as a suite?

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> I mean, we had the songs, we kind of&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;the last one, Zak recorded that song shortly before the Kamals.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> The one with the dog chained in the yard?

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> Yeah, and then the other two, we recorded kind of fast and on the spot because we wanted to put them on [the EP]; we’re really huge fans of hidden tracks.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> The reason I was confused is because, usually when you have a hidden track, it’s at the end of the album and it’s only a single track.

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> On the [physical] CD listing, it *is* at the end.

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> We kind of moved them around [for the digital download].

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> We do a lot of digital downloads, and it kept getting skipped [, so we rearranged them so people would download it. But the preferred running order is the one from the CD].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> <cite class="short-work">Hands in the Water</cite> seems to be about repentance, making amends for a transgression?

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> Little bit. Just trying to forgive yourself for something you did in the past, that I turned into a story about a convict.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> So, you actually had a pretty concrete story for the lyric?

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> I’m not really good at telling stories, and that song took a long time to get together […].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Who writes the lyrics?

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> We both do. [*gestures at Loren*]

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> What about the musical composition?

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> [It varies]. Whoever has a good idea; I mean, someone’s good idea might get trumped by someone’s better idea and no one gets upset about it.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> I thought that <cite class="short-work">Hands in the Water</cite> sounded a lot like early Doobie Brothers. Are they an influence?

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> Yeah, I like them before Michael McDonald.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Describe the band’s sound in thirty seconds: the classic elevator pitch to an A&R man (or woman).

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> <span class="h-card p-name">Otis Redding</span> meets <span class="h-card p-org">Black Sabbath</span> meets <span class="h-card p-org">Pink Floyd</span> meets <span class="h-card p-name">Jimi Hendrix</span>. Done!

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Anything else?

<span class="spkr">Allen</span> The term <q>heavy soul</q> was being [tossed around] for a while. Not just because of the <span class="h-card p-org">Black Keys</span> song. It felt good, sayin’ it.

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> Mainly because of the Otis Redding and Black Sabbath thing.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Care to name any other influences?

<span class="spkr">Allen</span> <span class="h-card p-org">Queens of the Stone Age</span>, […] <span class="h-card p-org">Led Zeppelin</span>.

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> For me, it changes from month to month. I’m on a big <span class="h-card p-org">ZZ Top</span> kick. Even ’though I don’t really listen to that much ZZ Top, I’ve got this idea of what [they] would do, you know, <q>Da da da da da da da da.</q> [*plays air guitar version of riff from* <cite class="short-work">La Grange</cite>.]

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Yeah, except that riff actually came from <span class="h-card p-name">John Lee Hooker</span> and <span class="h-card p-name">Slim Harpo</span>.

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> Old blues, for sure. I guess it’s the staccato, straight-and-to-the-point guitar. I like that a lot; I like psychedelic music&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> And by that, you mean 1960s to early ’70s psychedelic, not <span class="h-card p-org">Flaming Lips</span> psychedelic.

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> Yeah, I mean, they don’t influence my writing, but I don’t dislike the Flaming Lips. Then [I like] soul music&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;anything from <span class="h-card p-org">Stax Records</span>; I [also like] <span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">Muddy Waters</span>, John Lee Hooker, <span class="h-card p-name">Skip James</span>, <span class="h-card p-name">Robert Johnson</span>. And I like some new stuff, like <span class="h-card p-org">Zero 7</span>; spacey, electronic music; […] <span class="h-card p-org">Air</span> is cool; <span class="h-card p-org">Portishead</span>; the <span class="h-card p-org">Gorrilaz</span>. I like a lot of different types of music.

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> I like very creative chord progressions, as well as old <span class="h-card p-name p-nickname">Leadbelly</span>, <span class="h-card p-name">Charlie Patton</span>, <span class="h-card p-name">“Blind” Willie McTell</span>; things like that. My dad was all into the New Jack [Swing] and stuff like that, <span class="h-card p-org">Boyz 2 Men</span>; if you can get over the whole “boy band” thing, the writers behind it wrote really cool chord progressions. Even <span class="h-card p-name p-given-name">Seal</span> was a big influence [on me].

Say, you’ve got two sections of a song that don’t fit together at all, there’s always some weird bridge that transitions really well, and [that’s] always been really prominent to [the way I hear].

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> There’s a pretty audible sixty hertz hum on<cite class="short-work">Between the Tides</cite>; was that deliberate, or did it just happen in the studio?

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> It just happened. We’re not shooting for perfection; we just want to get the point across.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> A couple of the songs felt like demos, production-wise. I’m thinking of the two acoustic bonus tracks; you’ve got some studio chatter in there.

<span class="spkr">Allen</span> We’re big on analogue, too; it’s kinda nice, having a big, healthy hum on there.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Anything in the works?

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> We’ve got a new album coming out. It’s gonna be so awesome, I’m really excited. It’s called <cite>Dwellers</cite>; it’s a full-length [album]. [We’ve got] eight [tracks] so far; then if we can scrounge up another one, there’ll be nine, but eight for sure. Hopefully we can finish all the mixing and the tracking by Wednesday.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Are they all pop single length, or do you have any epic-length pieces on the album?

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> We’ve got some surprises in store.

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> There’s some epic-ness.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> How often do you perform?

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> We play at least two shows a month on average, but it ranges from no shows to seven shows a month.

<span class="spkr">Allen</span> Recently, we played in my buddy’s field; we set up some generators [and just had a party]. […] Soon as we get this album out, it’s gonna be full-on again.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Any tour plans?

<mpb-dialog-img>

{% image "./src/assets/images/blog/2013-02-11-kamals/czahller-20130112-23-10-46.jpg", "", "Trey plays bass. He wears a red bandana around his head. River is visible but out-of-focus to his right.", "Trey (II)", "large" %}</mpb-dialog-img>

<span class="spkr">Allen</span> We’d love too. [But,] we’re all really blue collar&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;we all work together, actually, at the same warehouse, so it’s kind of hard for us to just […] take off like that. We’re really gonna need some rocket fuel to get there. […] We’ve all worked for everything we have.

<span class="spkr">MPB</span> Redeem a song: pick a song that you like, a “guilty pleasure,” and tell me why it’s actually great and why I should like it.

<span class="spkr">Allen</span> Pretty much, the whole <span class="h-card p-org">Eagles</span> catalogue. [But] I really like <cite class="short-work">Lyin’ Eyes.</cite> […] Anything <span class="h-card p-name">Don Henley</span> influenced. But, uh, probably <cite class="short-work">New York Minute</cite>; my dad used to sing that song all the time […]. He really loves the Eagles; [they’re not a guilty pleasure] for him, but they are for me […].

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> I’m not really embarrassed by what I listen to.

<span class="spkr">Allen</span> What about something you wouldn’t want anyone to hear you singin’ in the shower?

<span class="spkr">Williams</span> Well, like, <span class="h-card p-name">Brian McNight</span>?&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;&mdash;&NoBreak;&hairsp;&NoBreak;or Seal: <cite class="short-work">Kiss from a Rose.</cite> Certain songs evoke emotions, and, I think, in music, that’s where it gets […] where people have their own opinions […]; the <span class="h-card p-org">Strokes</span> or <span class="h-card p-org">Linkin Park</span> might bring out certain emotions […] that I wouldn’t feel; I can’t expect everybody to feel a Seal song.

<span class="spkr">Kaczka</span> <span class="h-card p-org">Arctic Monkeys</span>, <cite class="short-work">Brianstorm.</cite> It’s not *Brainstorm*; it’s *Brianstorm*. It starts out, it’s just like, *toms*, crazy tom rolls, two or three bars; and then […] it stops, and then there’s just a single guitar line, really fast; the whole song’s really fast. It’s got really cool breaks in it; the guy[^6] can sing really well[; he’s] a really great lyricist. They’ve got cool changes; it’s not necessarily what you’d expect, but when they do go to those changes, it makes you feel good […].

[^6]: <span class="h-card p-name">Alex Turner</span>.
